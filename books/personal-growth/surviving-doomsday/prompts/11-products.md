# 11 — Products: personal projects, micro SaaS, internal tools

There are three product paths: **personal projects** (learning and proof of
work), **micro SaaS** (small paid products, often solo), and **internal SaaS**
(custom tools built for one company's own use, sold as a service or a retainer).
This track looks at how each one actually makes money, and the risks.

---

You are a bootstrapped founder who has shipped several small software products
(most failed and a few paid off) and has built internal tools for companies.
Research which of the three product paths fits an engineer like me, and how to
do it without wasting a year.

Work in `books/personal-growth/surviving-doomsday/` in this repo. Read its
`CLAUDE.md` first and follow it exactly. Then read `profile.md`,
`research/01-landscape/report.md`, and `research/05-domain/report.md` if they
exist. Use web search. Revenue claims from founders are C-grade unless verified,
because survivorship bias dominates this topic. Look for base rates.

## Questions to answer

1. **Base rates**: what does data say about how small software products actually
   fare? Revenue distributions, time to first dollar, failure rates. Use
   marketplace data and payment-provider reports where public, and say how
   reliable each one is.
2. **Platform risk**: when foundation-model providers ship a feature that
   replaces a "wrapper" product, what happens? Find documented cases. What makes
   a small product defensible? Consider proprietary data, workflow lock-in,
   distribution, domain depth, and integrations.
3. **Idea sourcing and validation**: how do you find problems worth paying for?
   Domain pain, your own work, client patterns. How do you validate before
   building: pre-sales, landing-page tests, concierge MVPs? Grade the evidence.
4. **Distribution beats building**: which channels work for micro SaaS? SEO,
   marketplaces and app stores, communities, partnerships, content. How does
   AI-driven search change SEO-based distribution?
5. **Internal tools path**: how do engineers sell custom internal tools or AI
   automations to companies? Pricing, ongoing maintenance, ownership of the
   code, and how this differs from consulting (track 07).
6. **Personal projects**: which ones count as proof of work and which are just
   tutorials with extra steps?
7. Operations reality: payments, taxes, support load, maintenance. Check what's
   available from my location (see `profile.md`) using official sources.
8. Kill criteria: when do you stop a product? Define them before starting.

## Avoid

- "$10k MRR in 30 days" stories as evidence.
- Recommending a specific idea without demand evidence.

## Output

Write `research/11-products/report.md` and `sources.md` per `CLAUDE.md`. Also
