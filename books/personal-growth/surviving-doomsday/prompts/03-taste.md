# 03 — Taste

"Taste is the new moat" is a popular claim. This track tests whether it's true
and, if so, how taste is actually built.

---

You are a product designer and a principal engineer who both distrust slogans.
Research what taste is in software and product work, and whether and how it
can be developed.

Work in `books/personal-growth/surviving-doomsday/` in this repo. Read its
`CLAUDE.md` first and follow it exactly. Then read `profile.md` and
`research/01-landscape/report.md` if they exist. Use web search.

## Questions to answer

1. Define taste operationally for engineers. Cover taste in code, APIs,
   architecture, products, and writing. What does someone with taste do
   differently, in ways you can observe?
2. Is taste trainable? What does research on aesthetic judgement and expertise
   (perceptual learning, the role of exposure and critique) say? Separate the
   findings from practitioner lore.
3. What practices do respected builders describe for developing taste? Examples:
   studying great codebases, copywork, critique, building many small things,
   killing your own work. Treat these as B/C-grade evidence and grade them.
4. Test the claim: "when generation is cheap, curation and taste become the
   bottleneck." Find evidence for and against it. Is taste becoming more
   valuable, or is AI making "good enough" the norm and taste a luxury?
5. How do you show taste to employers and clients, who can't see it directly?
6. Failure modes: taste as gatekeeping, taste without shipping, mistaking
   familiarity for quality.

## Avoid

- Quote-mining famous founders as proof.
- Treating "taste" as unexplainable.

## Output

Write `research/03-taste/report.md` and `sources.md` per `CLAUDE.md`. In
"What to do", include a 12-week taste curriculum: what to study, what to make,
how to get critique, and how to tell it's working. Commit:
`research(surviving-doomsday/03-taste): first pass`.
