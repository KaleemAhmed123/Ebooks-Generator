# 05 — Domain knowledge

Generic coding is the most automatable layer. Knowing a business domain deeply
(finance, health, logistics, law, energy...) and being able to code in it is
much harder to replace. This track finds which domains pay off and how to get
deep fast.

---

You are an industry analyst who places engineers into specialized sectors.
Research which domain specializations pay off for software/AI engineers, and how
to acquire domain depth fast.

Work in `books/personal-growth/surviving-doomsday/` in this repo. Read its
`CLAUDE.md` first and follow it exactly. Then read `profile.md` and
`research/01-landscape/report.md` if they exist. Use web search, job-posting
data, and industry reports.

## Questions to answer

1. What evidence shows domain-plus-software profiles earn more or are more
   resilient? Look at pay data, job postings, and hiring-manager reports.
2. Which domains have the most unmet software and AI demand right now, and why?
   Consider regulation, legacy systems, data access, trust requirements, and
   slow-moving incumbents.
3. Is regulation a moat? Compliance-heavy domains like health, finance, and
   legal are harder for generic AI products to enter. Look for evidence for
   and against.
4. How do engineers acquire domain depth fast? Embedding with operators, reading
   the domain's own literature and regulations, certifications that matter,
   apprenticing on real problems. Which certifications have real signal and
   which are noise?
5. Using `profile.md`, shortlist 3–5 domains that fit my existing edges and
   market. Score each on demand, my fit, time to credibility, and AI-automation
   risk.
6. What's the risk of specializing too early, and how do you hedge it?

## Avoid

- "Pick your passion" advice with no market data.
- Domains chosen because they're trendy this month.

## Output

Write `research/05-domain/report.md` and `sources.md` per `CLAUDE.md`. In
"What to do", include a 90-day domain-immersion plan for the top-scored domain.
Commit: `research(surviving-doomsday/05-domain): first pass`.
