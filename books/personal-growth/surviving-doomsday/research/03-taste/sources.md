## Perception, expertise and aesthetic judgement

[S1] Is Spacing the "Enemy of Induction"? — Kornell & Bjork — Psychological Science, 2008 — https://bjorklab.psych.ucla.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/13/2016/07/Kornell_Bjork_2008_PsychScience.pdf — study — A
     Interleaving paintings by different artists beat blocking them for learning each artist's style; learners still rated blocking as better.

[S2] Mere Exposure to Bad Art — Meskin, Phelan, Moore & Kieran — British Journal of Aesthetics 53(2):139–164, 2013 — https://blog.oup.com/2013/07/what-makes-art-bad-exposure-effect/ — study — A
     Repeated exposure raised liking for Millais but lowered it for Kinkade: exposure amplifies real quality differences rather than creating liking from nothing.

[S3] Gustave Caillebotte, French Impressionism, and Mere Exposure — Cutting — Psychonomic Bulletin & Review, 2003 — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_E._Cutting — study — A
     Covert repeated exposure shifted preferences among comparable Impressionist paintings; canons are partly maintained by how often images are shown.

[S4] Conditions for Intuitive Expertise: A Failure to Disagree — Kahneman & Klein — American Psychologist, Sept 2009 — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19739881/ — review — A
     Intuition is trustworthy only in environments with valid cues and chances to learn them; subjective confidence does not indicate accuracy.

[S5] Perceptual and adaptive learning technology in medical education — Kellman & Krasne — 2018 — https://kellmanlab.psych.ucla.edu/files/kellman_krasne_2018.pdf — review — A
     Pattern-recognition skills (dermatology, pathology, echocardiography) can be trained systematically with many classified examples and adaptive sequencing.

[S6] Sexing Day-Old Chicks: A Case Study and Expert Systems Analysis of a Difficult Perceptual-Learning Task — Biederman & Shiffrar — J. Exp. Psych.: LMC 13(4):640–645, 1987 — https://web-archive.southampton.ac.uk/cogprints.org/3255/index.html (cited via Horsey 2002) — study — A
     Experts could not say how they judged; once the key shape cue was made explicit, novices' accuracy rose sharply. Exact figures not verified here.

[S7] Deliberate Practice and Performance in Music, Games, Sports, Education, and Professions: A Meta-Analysis — Macnamara, Hambrick & Oswald — Psychological Science 25(8), 2014 — https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4101876/ — meta-analysis — A
     Practice explained 26% of performance variance in games, 21% music, 18% sports, 4% education, under 1% professions.

[S8] The Effects of Feedback Interventions on Performance — Kluger & DeNisi — Psychological Bulletin 119:254–284, 1996 — https://scholars.huji.ac.il/node/1802 — meta-analysis — A
     607 effect sizes: feedback helps on average (d = .41) but over a third of interventions lowered performance, mostly when feedback pulled attention to the self.

[S9] Peer and Self Assessment in Massive Online Classes — Kulkarni et al. — ACM TOCHI, 2013 — https://hci.stanford.edu/publications/paper.php?id=274 — study — A
     Peer design critique tracked staff grades closely with good rubrics; rubric revision cut median error from 12.4% to 9.9%; peers rated same-country work 3.6% higher.

[S10] Parallel Prototyping Leads to Better Design Results, More Divergence, and Increased Self-Efficacy — Dow et al. — ACM TOCHI 17(4), Dec 2010 — https://hci.stanford.edu/publications/2010/parallel-prototyping/ParallelPrototyping2010-submitted.pdf — experiment — A
     Novices who made several ad designs before critique beat those who iterated one at a time, by click-through and expert ratings.

[S11] An Examination of Judge Reliability at a Major U.S. Wine Competition — Hodgson — Journal of Wine Economics, 2008 — https://resolve.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-wine-economics/article/abs/an-examination-of-judge-reliability-at-a-major-us-wine-competition/15EF999FBAC63C5FB6DCF1C2F4BB6655 — study — A
     Same wine poured three times: about 10% of judges stayed within one medal band; about 10% sometimes scored it Bronze to Gold.

[S12] Learning a Metric for Code Readability — Buse & Weimer — IEEE TSE, 2010 — https://web.eecs.umich.edu/%7Eweimerw/p/weimer-tse2010-readability-preprint.pdf — study — A
     120 annotators rated 100 snippets; a model on simple local features predicted their judgements and correlated with change and defect measures.

[S16] Expertise in Design: An Overview — Cross — Design Studies 25(5):427–441, 2004 — https://oro.open.ac.uk/3271 — review — A
     Outstanding designers frame problems actively and scope them adequately rather than analysing exhaustively.

## Software quality and review

[S13] Code Red: The Business Impact of Code Quality — Tornhill & Borg — TechDebt 2022 (39 proprietary codebases) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.04374 — study — B (authors from the vendor whose metric is used)
     Low-health code had 15x more defects, 124% longer development time, and 9x longer maximum cycle times.

[S14] Expectations, Outcomes, and Challenges of Modern Code Review — Bacchelli & Bird — ICSE 2013 — https://2013.icse-conferences.org/content/expectations-outcomes-and-challenges-modern-code-review.html — study — A
     At Microsoft, review comments were mostly about code improvements (readability, consistency, dead code), and review's real yield was knowledge transfer more than defects.

[S15] The Pushback Effects of Race, Ethnicity, Gender, and Age in Code Review — Murphy-Hill et al. — CACM 65, 2022 — https://research.google/pubs/the-pushback-effects-of-race-ethnicity-gender-and-age-in-code-review/ — study — A
     At Google, review pushback varied by author demographics, costing an estimated 1,000+ engineer-hours per day.

[S26] AI Copilot Code Quality 2025 — GitClear — Jan/Feb 2025 — https://devclass.com/2025/02/20/ai-is-eroding-code-quality-states-new-in-depth-report/ — vendor report — C
     211M changed lines 2020–2024: duplicated 5+ line blocks up about 8x in 2024; moved/refactored lines fell from about 25% (2021) to under 10% (2024).

## AI, generation and curation

[S17] Generative Artificial Intelligence, Human Creativity, and Art — Zhou & Lee — PNAS Nexus, 5 Mar 2024 — https://www.bu.edu/questrom/blog/eric-zhou-dokyun-lee-generative-artificial-intelligence-human-creativity-and-art — study (observational, 4M+ artworks, 50k+ users) — A
     AI adopters' output rose 25% and favourites-per-view 50%; average novelty fell; artists who explored ideas and filtered outputs gained most.

[S18] Generative AI Enhances Individual Creativity but Reduces the Collective Diversity of Novel Content — Doshi & Hauser — Science Advances, 2024 — https://scale.stanford.edu/ai/repository/generative-ai-enhances-individual-creativity-reduces-collective-diversity-novel — experiment — A
     AI story ideas lifted the least creative writers to par with the most creative, and made stories more similar to each other.

[S19] Experimental Evidence on the Productivity Effects of Generative AI — Noy & Zhang — Science, 2023 — https://economics.mit.edu/sites/default/files/inline-files/Noy_Zhang_1_0.pdf — preregistered experiment (n=453) — A
     ChatGPT cut writing time 40% and raised quality 18%, compressed the gap between workers, and shifted work toward ideation and editing.

[S20] Generative AI at Work — Brynjolfsson, Li & Raymond — Quarterly Journal of Economics, 2025 — https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.11771v1 — field study (5,172 agents) — A
     +14% productivity on average, +34% for novices, minimal effect for the most skilled.

[S21] Navigating the Jagged Technological Frontier — Dell'Acqua et al. — Organization Science (orig. HBS WP 2023) — https://mitsloan.mit.edu/node/51095 — experiment (758 BCG consultants) — A
     Inside AI's capability frontier performance rose up to 40%; on a task outside it, AI users were 19 points less likely to be correct.

[S22] Measuring the Impact of Early-2025 AI on Experienced Open-Source Developer Productivity — METR — July 2025 — https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.09089 — RCT (16 devs, 246 tasks) — A
     AI-allowed tasks took 19% longer; developers forecast a 24% speedup beforehand.

[S23] We Are Changing Our Developer Productivity Experiment Design — METR — 6 Mar 2026 — https://conffab.com/?p=10163 (mirror of METR post) — research note — B
     Late-2025 follow-up pointed to speedups, but METR judged the data unreliable due to developers refusing to work without AI.

[S24] DORA 2025 State of AI-assisted Software Development — Google DORA — Sept 2025 — https://research.google/pubs/dora-2025-state-of-ai-assisted-software-development-report/ — survey (~5,000 respondents) — B
     AI acts as an amplifier: it magnifies strong organisations' strengths and weak ones' dysfunction.

[S25] Google DORA Research: Software Developers Use AI 'Heavily' — TechRepublic — Sept 2025 — https://www.techrepublic.com/article/news-dora-ai-report-2025/ — reporting — B
     Reports DORA 2025 figures: 90% AI adoption; 30% trust AI output "a little" or "not at all".

[S27] Amazon adds to KDP generative AI policy, caps daily self-publishing uploads — Authors Guild — Sept 2023 — https://authorsguild.org/news/amazon-adds-to-kdp-generative-ai-policy-caps-daily-self-publishing-uploads/ — reporting — B
     Amazon capped KDP at three new titles per day per account after a flood of suspected AI-written books.

## Hiring and signalling

[S28] Revisiting Meta-Analytic Estimates of Validity in Personnel Selection — Sackett, Zhang, Berry & Lievens — Journal of Applied Psychology, 2022 — https://ink.library.smu.edu.sg/lkcsb_research/6894 — meta-analysis — A
     Structured interviews top the corrected ranking (~.42); work samples ~.33; cognitive ability ~.31. Figures confirmed via secondary summaries only.

[S40] How to hire engineers for strong judgment and taste — LeadDev — accessed 2026-10-03 — https://leaddev.com/hiring/how-to-hire-engineers-for-strong-judgment-and-taste — practitioner article — C
     Hiring managers report probing taste through open-ended take-homes and asking candidates to explain decisions and cases where the AI was wrong.
