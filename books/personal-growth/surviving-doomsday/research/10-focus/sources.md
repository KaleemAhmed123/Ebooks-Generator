## Interruptions and task switching

[S1] The Cost of Interrupted Work: More Speed and Stress — Mark, Gudith, Klocke (CHI '08) — 2008-04 — https://www.ics.uci.edu/~gmark/chi08-mark.pdf — study (lab experiment) — Grade A
     Interrupted email tasks were finished faster with no quality loss, at the price of more stress, frustration, time pressure, and effort.

[S2] Momentary interruptions can derail the train of thought — Altmann, Trafton, Hambrick, J. Exp. Psychol.: General 143(1):215–226 — 2014 — https://psnet.ahrq.gov/issue/momentary-interruptions-can-derail-train-thought — study (lab experiment) — Grade A
     Interruptions averaging 2.8 s doubled sequence errors; 4.4 s tripled them, on a procedural task.

[S3] No Task Left Behind? Examining the Nature of Fragmented Work — Mark, Gonzalez, Harris (CHI '05) — 2005-04 — https://isr.uci.edu/node/1329.html — study (field observation, 24 information workers) — Grade A
     57% of "working spheres" were interrupted; most interrupted work resumed the same day, after more than two intervening activities.

[S4] Too Many Interruptions at Work? (interview with Gloria Mark) — Gallup Business Journal — 2006 — https://news.gallup.com/businessjournal/23146/too-many-interruptions-work.aspx — interview — Grade B
     Origin of the "23 minutes 15 seconds" figure: average time before returning to an interrupted working sphere, in field observation of 36 workers over 3 days.

[S5] Resumption Strategies for Interrupted Programming Tasks — Parnin, Rugaber, Software Quality Journal — 2011 — https://sites.cc.gatech.edu/reverse/repository/resumptionstrategies.pdf — study (10,000 recorded sessions, 85 programmers) — Grade A
     Only ~10% of sessions had an edit within a minute of resuming; programmers rebuild context by navigating code and running the program.

[S6] Why is it so hard to do my work? The challenge of attention residue when switching between work tasks — Leroy, Organizational Behavior and Human Decision Processes 109(2) — 2009-07 — https://strategy-business.com/article/re00088 — study (lab experiments) — Grade A
     Defines attention residue; finishing Task A, or closing it under time pressure, reduces residue on Task B.

[S7] Task switching — Monsell, Trends in Cognitive Sciences 7(3):134–140 — 2003-03 — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12639695/ — review — Grade A
     Switch costs are reliable; preparation reduces but does not eliminate them (residual cost).

[S8] Software Developers' Perceptions of Productivity — Meyer, Fritz, Murphy, Zimmermann (FSE '14) — 2014-11 — https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/software-developers-perceptions-of-productivity/ — study (survey n=379 + observation n=11) — Grade A
     Developers call a day productive when they finish tasks without significant interruptions, yet observed developers switched often and still felt productive.

[S9] Attention Span — Gloria Mark (book, Hanover Square Press) — 2023 — https://www.brainhq.com/books/attention-span/ — book by the primary researcher — Grade B
     Reports her measured average time on one screen before switching: ~2.5 min (2004), ~75 s (later), ~47 s (recent).

[S10] Cognitive control in media multitaskers: Two replication studies and a meta-analysis — Wiradhany, Nieuwenstein, Attention, Perception & Psychophysics — 2017 — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5662702 — replication + meta-analysis — Grade A
     Pooled media-multitasking/distractibility link d = 0.17, non-significant after small-study-bias correction; 26 of 39 tests null.

## Interventions

[S11] Batching smartphone notifications can improve well-being — Fitz, Kushlev, Jagannathan, Lewis, Paliwal, Ariely, Computers in Human Behavior 101:84–94 — 2019-12 — https://scholars.duke.edu/publication/1402953 — study (field RCT, n=237, 2 weeks) — Grade A
     Batching 3×/day improved attention, productivity, mood, and sense of control; no notifications at all raised anxiety and FoMO.

[S12] Checking email less frequently reduces stress — Kushlev, Dunn, Computers in Human Behavior — 2015 — https://www.psypost.org/resist-temptation-check-email-less-reduce-stress-study-finds/ — study (within-subject field experiment, n=124) — Grade A
     Limiting email to 3 checks/day for a week lowered daily stress vs unlimited checking.

[S13] Digital Addiction — Allcott, Gentzkow, Song, American Economic Review 112(7):2424–2463 — 2022 — https://www.nber.org/papers/w28936 — study (RCT, ~2,000 adults) — Grade A
     A self-set screen-time limit tool had high take-up and cut use; self-control problems estimated at 31% of social media use; temporary incentives had persistent effects (habit formation).

[S14] The Welfare Effects of Social Media — Allcott, Braghieri, Eichmeyer, Gentzkow, American Economic Review — 2020 — https://www.aeaweb.org/doi/10.1257/aer.20190658 — study (RCT, 2,743 recruited) — Grade A
     Four weeks off Facebook raised subjective well-being.

[S15] Implementation intentions and goal achievement: a meta-analysis — Gollwitzer, Sheeran, Advances in Experimental Social Psychology 38:69–119 — 2006 — https://www.socmot.uni-konstanz.de/publications/implementation-intentions-and-goal-achievement-meta-analysis-effects-and-processes — meta-analysis (94 tests) — Grade A
     If-then plans: d = 0.65 on goal attainment, including shielding ongoing goals from distraction.

[S16] How are habits formed: Modelling habit formation in the real world — Lally, van Jaarsveld, Potts, Wardle, European Journal of Social Psychology — 2010 — https://www.ucl.ac.uk/news/2009/aug/how-long-does-it-take-form-habit — study (n=96, 84 days) — Grade A
     Median 66 days to automaticity plateau, range 18–254 (extrapolated); early repetitions add the most.

[S17] Does time management work? A meta-analysis — Aeon, Faber, Panaccio, PLOS ONE 16(1):e0245066 — 2021 — https://ideas.repec.org/a/plo/pone00/0245066.html — meta-analysis (158 studies, 53,000+ people) — Grade A
     Time management relates moderately to performance and more strongly to well-being; correlational.

[S18] Consider it done! Plan making can eliminate the cognitive effects of unfulfilled goals — Masicampo, Baumeister, J. Personality and Social Psychology — 2011 — https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/blog/dont-delay/201111/the-power-of-a-plan — study (lab experiments) — Grade A (finding) / B (summary used)
     Making a specific plan for an unfinished goal removed its intrusive thoughts and interference, without doing the task.

[S19] Brain Drain: mere presence of one's own smartphone — replication record — FORRT FLoRA Replication Atlas (Ward et al. 2017; Ruiz Pardo & Minda 2022) — accessed 2026-10-03 — https://forrt.org/flora-replication-atlas/doi/10.1086/691462/ — replication database — Grade A
     Two recorded replications of the "phone presence drains capacity" effect, both failed.

## Deep work and practice

[S20] Deliberate practice and performance in music, games, sports, education, and professions: a meta-analysis — Macnamara, Hambrick, Oswald, Psychological Science 25(8):1608–1618 — 2014 — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4101876/ — meta-analysis — Grade A
     Deliberate practice explains 26% of variance (games), 21% (music), 18% (sports), 4% (education), <1% (professions).

[S21] The role of deliberate practice in expert performance: revisiting Ericsson, Krampe & Tesch-Römer (1993) — Macnamara, Maitra, Royal Society Open Science — 2019 — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6731745 — replication (n=39, double-blind) — Grade A
     Did not replicate the clean practice→skill gradient; practice explained 26% of variance vs 48% originally.

## Anxiety, job insecurity, coping

[S22] No security: a meta-analysis and review of job insecurity and its consequences — Sverke, Hellgren, Näswall, J. Occupational Health Psychology 7:242–264 — 2002 — https://www.authorea.com/users/105190/articles/146424/master/file/No_security_A_meta_analysis__.md — meta-analysis — Grade A
     Job insecurity harms job attitudes, health, and to some extent work behaviour.

[S23] Cognitive and Affective Job Insecurity: A Meta-Analysis and a Primary Study — Jiang, Lavaysse, Journal of Management — 2018 — https://researchspace.auckland.ac.nz/handle/2292/43594 — meta-analysis (535 samples, 58 outcomes) — Grade A
     Insecurity linked to 51 of 58 negative outcomes; the affective part (worry, fear) predicts outcomes more strongly than the cognitive part (perceived threat).

[S24] The influence of fear on risk taking: a meta-analysis — Wake, Wormwood, Satpute, Cognition & Emotion 34(6):1143–1159 — 2020 — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7423744 — meta-analysis (136 effects, 9,544 people) — Grade A
     Fear/anxiety → less risk-taking and higher risk estimates, r = 0.22; larger with real stakes and clinical anxiety.

[S25] Threat-rigidity effects in organizational behavior — Staw, Sandelands, Dutton, Administrative Science Quarterly — 1981 — https://www.kellogg.northwestern.edu/research/ktag/resources/papers/kennedy.pdf — theory/review — Grade B
     Under threat, people and organizations narrow information processing and fall back on well-learned responses.

[S26] Media's role in broadcasting acute stress following the Boston Marathon bombings — Holman, Garfin, Silver, PNAS — 2013-12 (vol. 111, 2014) — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3890785 — study (representative survey, n=4,675) — Grade A
     Repeated daily media exposure was associated with more acute stress than being at the event.

[S27] Effects of occupational stress management intervention programs: a meta-analysis — Richardson, Rothstein, J. Occupational Health Psychology — 2008 — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18211170/ — meta-analysis (36 studies, 55 interventions) — Grade A
     Overall d = 0.526; cognitive-behavioural programs produced consistently larger effects than other types.

[S28] A systematic review and meta-analysis of workplace mindfulness training RCTs — Bartlett et al., J. Occupational Health Psychology 24(1):108–126 — 2019 — https://research-portal.uea.ac.uk/en/publications/a-systematic-review-and-meta-analysis-of-workplace-mindfulness-tr/ — meta-analysis (23 RCTs) — Grade A
     Stress g = 0.56, anxiety g = 0.62; no conclusion possible for work performance or burnout.

[S29] Effectiveness of physical activity interventions for improving depression, anxiety and distress: an umbrella review — Singh et al., British Journal of Sports Medicine 57(18):1203 — 2023 — https://bjsm.bmj.com/content/57/18/1203 — umbrella review (1,039 trials) — Grade A
     Medium effects on anxiety (median ES −0.42) and distress (−0.60); higher intensity helped more.

[S30] A preliminary investigation of stimulus control training for worry — McGowan, Behar, Behavior Modification — 2013 — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11303915/ (context in later RCT) — study (RCT, n=46) — Grade A
     A daily 30-min fixed worry period beat a control on worry, anxiety, and insomnia in two weeks; later work found weaker support in diagnosed GAD.

[S31] Generalised anxiety disorder in adults — NHS — accessed 2026-10-03 — https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/generalised-anxiety-disorder/ — official health guidance — Grade A
     See a doctor when anxiety is present most of the time and affects daily life; CBT is first-line; UK adults can self-refer to talking therapies.

[S32] AI anxiety research summaries (ADP global survey, 2026) — Fortune — 2026-03-25 — https://fortune.com/2026/03/25/workers-anxious-scared-insecure-ai-adp-global-survey/ — reporting on a vendor survey — Grade C
     Survey-level prevalence of AI job fear; used only as context, not for a conclusion.

## Market signal vs noise

[S33] Canaries in the Coal Mine? Six Facts about the Recent Employment Effects of AI — Brynjolfsson, Chandar, Chen, Stanford Digital Economy Lab (working paper, rev. 2025-11-13) — 2025 — https://digitaleconomy.stanford.edu/publication/canaries-in-the-coal-mine/ — study (payroll administrative data) — Grade A (data) / not peer-reviewed
     Ages 22–25 in the most AI-exposed occupations: 16% relative employment decline vs less exposed, controlling for firm shocks; older workers stable.

[S34] Canaries, interest rates, and timing — Stanford Digital Economy Lab — 2026-02-09 — https://digitaleconomy.stanford.edu/news/canaries-interest-rates-and-timinga-more-on-recent-drivers-of-employment-changes-for-young-workers — data note — Grade A
     Interest rates do not explain the disproportionate drop in entry-level hiring in AI-exposed occupations.

[S35] Evaluating the impact of AI on the labor market: current state of affairs — Yale Budget Lab with Brookings — 2025-10 — https://budgetlab.yale.edu/research/evaluating-impact-ai-labor-market-current-state-affairs — data analysis — Grade A
     No "discernible disruption" in the overall occupational mix since ChatGPT; shifts predate it.

[S36] Measuring the Impact of Early-2025 AI on Experienced Open-Source Developer Productivity — METR — 2025-07 — https://simonwillison.net/2025/Jul/12/ai-open-source-productivity/ (summary of the METR paper) — study (RCT, 16 devs, 246 tasks) — Grade A (small n)
     Devs took 19% longer with AI while predicting 24% faster and still believing afterwards they were ~20% faster.

[S37] Anthropic CEO: AI writing 90% of code in 3–6 months — Business Insider (reporting CFR remarks) — 2025-03 — https://www.businessinsider.nl/anthropics-ceo-says-that-in-3-to-6-months-ai-will-be-writing-90-of-the-code-software-developers-were-in-charge-of/ — reporting of a lab-CEO prediction — Grade B (reporting) / C (claim)
     Example of a dated, falsifiable timeline claim from an interested party.

[S38] Is 90% of code at Anthropic being written by AIs? — Redwood Research blog — 2025 — https://blog.redwoodresearch.org/p/is-90-of-code-at-anthropic-being — analysis — Grade B
     Examines the claim after the deadline and finds it unclear what was measured or whether it held.

[S39] Digital News Report 2025 — Reuters Institute for the Study of Journalism — 2025-06 — https://digitalnewsreport.org/?p=6115 — survey data (~100,000 people, 48 markets) — Grade A
     4 in 10 avoid news at least sometimes; 39% of avoiders cite the effect on mood, 31% being worn out by the volume.

## Myths

[S40] Dopamine fasting: Misunderstanding science spawns a maladaptive fad — Harvard Health Publishing — 2020-02 — https://www.health.harvard.edu/blog/dopamine-fasting-misunderstanding-science-spawns-a-maladaptive-fad-2020022618917 — expert commentary — Grade B
     Avoiding stimulation does not lower dopamine; the originator (Sepah) says the name is not literal and the method is CBT stimulus control.
