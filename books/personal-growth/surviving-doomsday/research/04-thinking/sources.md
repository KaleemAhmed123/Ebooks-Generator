## Raising IQ / brain training

[S1] Working memory training does not improve performance on measures of intelligence or other measures of "far transfer": evidence from a meta-analytic review — Melby-Lervåg, Redick, Hulme — 2016 — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4968033 — study (meta-analysis, 87 publications, 145 comparisons) — Grade A
     WM training gives near transfer to WM tasks; no reliable far transfer vs. active controls; WM gains don't predict far-transfer gains.

[S2] Near and far transfer in cognitive training: a second-order meta-analysis — Sala, Aksayli, Tatlidil, Tatsumi, Gondo, Gobet (Collabra: Psychology) — 2019 — https://pure.fujita-hu.ac.jp/en/publications/near-and-far-transfer-in-cognitive-training-a-second-order-meta-a/ — study (meta-analysis of meta-analyses) — Grade A
     After controlling for placebo effects and publication bias, far-transfer effect size and true variance are zero across populations and programs. (Read via abstract.)

[S3] Do "brain-training" programs work? — Simons, Boot, Charness, Gathercole, Chabris, Hambrick, Stine-Morrow (Psychological Science in the Public Interest 17(3)) — 2016 — https://www.psychologicalscience.org/publications/brain-training.html — study (systematic review, 130+ papers) — Grade A
     Trained-task gains are real; little evidence for distant transfer or everyday cognition; many vendor-cited studies poorly designed.

[S4] Lumosity to pay $2 million to settle FTC deceptive advertising charges for its "brain training" program — US Federal Trade Commission — 2016-01-05 — https://ftc.gov/news-events/press-releases/2016/01/lumosity-pay-2-million-settle-ftc-deceptive-advertising-charges — official record — Grade A
     Regulator found claims of better work/school performance and delayed cognitive decline unsupported.

[S5] Improving fluid intelligence with training on working memory: a meta-analysis — Au, Sheehan, Tsai, Duncan, Buschkuehl, Jaeggi (Psychonomic Bulletin & Review) — 2015 — https://www.doi.org/10.3758/S13423-014-0699-X — study (meta-analysis, 20 studies) — Grade A
     The pro-transfer side: n-back training → fluid intelligence, g = 0.24. (Effect size via abstract/secondary summaries.)

[S6] How much does education improve intelligence? A meta-analysis — Ritchie, Tucker-Drob (Psychological Science) — 2018 — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6088505/ — study (meta-analysis, 42 datasets, 600,000+ participants) — Grade A
     Each extra year of schooling ≈ +1 to +5 IQ points (pooled ≈ 3.4), lasting into old age.

[S7] Speed-of-processing training with boosters and 20-year dementia diagnoses (ACTIVE trial, Alzheimer's & Dementia: TRCI) — reported by ReachMD / Patient Care — 2026-02-09 — https://reachmd.com/news/speed-focused-cognitive-training-with-booster-sessions-linked-to-lower-dementia-diagnoses-over-20-years/2485634/ — news on an RCT follow-up — Grade B
     Speed training + boosters: 40% vs 49% claims-based dementia diagnoses over ~20 years; memory and reasoning training showed no significant effect. The commercial version of the task is sold by BrainHQ (interest noted).

## Learning techniques

[S8] Rethinking the use of tests: a meta-analysis of practice testing — Adesope, Trevisan, Sundararajan (Review of Educational Research 87(3)) — 2017 — https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.3102/0034654316689306 — study (meta-analysis, 118 articles, 272 effect sizes, 15,427 participants) — Grade A
     Practice testing beats other study conditions, g = 0.61.

[S9] Improving students' learning with effective learning techniques — Dunlosky, Rawson, Marsh, Nathan, Willingham (Psychological Science in the Public Interest) — 2013 — https://www.psychologicalscience.org/news/releases/which-study-strategies-make-the-grade.html — study (review of 10 techniques) — Grade A
     Practice testing and distributed practice rated high utility; rereading, highlighting, summarization rated low.

[S10] Distributed practice in verbal recall tasks: a review and quantitative synthesis — Cepeda, Pashler, Vul, Wixted, Rohrer (Psychological Bulletin 132) — 2006 — https://www.evullab.org/pdf/CepedaPashlerVulWixtedRohrer-PB-2006.pdf — study (meta-analysis, 839 assessments, 317 experiments) — Grade A
     Spacing beats massing; the optimal gap grows with the retention interval.

[S11] Spacing effects in learning: a temporal ridgeline of optimal retention — Cepeda, Vul, Rohrer, Wixted, Pashler (Psychological Science) — 2008 — https://www.evullab.org/pdf/CepedaVulRohrerWixtedPashler-PS-2008.pdf — study (1,350+ participants) — Grade A
     Optimal gap ≈ 20–40% of a 1-week retention interval, ≈ 5–10% of a 1-year interval.

[S12] Inducing self-explanation: a meta-analysis — Bisra, Liu, Nesbit, Salimi, Winne (Educational Psychology Review) — 2018 — https://www.Gwern.net/doc/psychology/spaced-repetition/2018-bisra.pdf — study (meta-analysis, 64 reports, ~5,900 learners) — Grade A
     Self-explanation prompts, g = 0.55.

[S13] Similarity matters: a meta-analysis of interleaved learning and its moderators — Brunmair, Richter (Psychological Bulletin) — 2019 — https://www.psychologie.uni-wuerzburg.de/fileadmin/06020400/2019/Brunmair_Richter_in_press__2019_META-ANALYSIS_OF_INTERLEAVED_LEARNING.pdf — study (meta-analysis, 59 studies, 238 effects) — Grade A
     Interleaving g = 0.42 overall; 0.34 for math; works best when categories are confusable; reverses for word lists.

[S14] A meta-analysis of the worked examples effect on mathematics performance — Barbieri, Miller-Cotto, Clerjuste, Chawla (Educational Psychology Review 35) — 2023 — https://oaks.kent.edu/hcri/meta-analysis-worked-examples-effect-mathematics-performance — study (meta-analysis, 55 studies, 181 effects) — Grade A
     Worked examples ≈ +18 percentile points vs. problem solving; adding self-explanation prompts to worked examples did worse.

[S15] The expertise reversal effect — Kalyuga, Ayres, Chandler, Sweller (Educational Psychologist 38(1)) and later reviews — 2003 — https://my.chartered.college/impact_article/expertise-reversal-effect-and-its-instructional-implications/ — study/review — Grade A (read via a review summary)
     Guidance that helps novices (worked examples) stops helping or hurts learners who already have the schemas.

[S16] Learning by preparing-to-teach and teaching: a meta-analysis — Kobayashi (Japanese Psychological Research) — 2019 — https://metatoc.com/papers/112234-learning-by-preparing-to-teach-and-teaching-a-meta-analysis — study (meta-analysis, 28 studies) — Grade A
     Preparing to teach g = 0.35; preparing + actually teaching g = 0.56; larger when teaching is interactive.

[S17] The effects of school-based writing-to-learn interventions on academic achievement — Bangert-Drowns, Hurley, Wilkinson (Review of Educational Research 74) — 2004 — https://openjournals.libs.uga.edu/tme/article/download/2004/1909/3169 — study (meta-analysis, 48 studies) — Grade A
     Writing-to-learn effect 0.17; larger with metacognitive prompts; longer assignments did not help.

[S18] Deliberate practice and performance in music, games, sports, education, and professions: a meta-analysis — Macnamara, Hambrick, Oswald (Psychological Science 25(8)) — 2014 — https://www.psychologicalscience.org/news/releases/becoming-an-expert-takes-more-than-practice.html — study (meta-analysis, 88 studies) — Grade A
     Practice explains 26% of variance in games, 21% music, 18% sports, 4% education, <1% professions.

[S19] Effect of prior knowledge on good and poor readers' memory of text — Recht, Leslie (Journal of Educational Psychology 80(1)) — 1988 — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baseball_Study — study (n = 64) — Grade A (small; read via summaries)
     Poor readers with high baseball knowledge outperformed good readers with low knowledge.

[S20] Perception in chess — Chase, Simon (Cognitive Psychology) — 1973; Gobet & Simon replication 1996 — https://www.Gwern.net/doc/psychology/chess/1996-gobet.pdf — study — Grade A
     Expert recall advantage comes from stored patterns ("chunks"); it largely vanishes on random positions.

## Sleep, exercise, stress

[S21] A meta-analysis of the impact of short-term sleep deprivation on cognitive variables — Lim, Dinges (Psychological Bulletin 136(3)) — 2010 — https://www.med.upenn.edu/uep/assets/user-content/documents/LimDinges2010MetaAnalysis.pdf — study (meta-analysis, 70 articles, 147 tests) — Grade A
     ≤48 h total sleep loss: simple-attention lapses g = −0.78; reasoning accuracy g = −0.13 (non-significant).

[S22] The cumulative cost of additional wakefulness — Van Dongen, Maislin, Mullington, Dinges (Sleep 26(2)) — 2003 — https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20040087752 — study (n = 48, controlled lab) — Grade A
     14 nights of 6 h sleep ≈ one night of total sleep loss on lapses and working memory; subjects did not notice.

[S23] An umbrella review of randomized control trials on the effects of physical exercise on cognition — Ciria et al. (Nature Human Behaviour 7) — 2023 — https://ideas.repec.org/a/nat/nathum/v7y2023i6d10.1038_s41562-023-01554-4.html — study (umbrella review + re-meta-analysis) — Grade A
     Healthy people: d = 0.22 raw → 0.13 after moderators → 0.05 (CrI −0.09 to 0.14) after publication-bias correction.

[S24] Effectiveness of exercise for improving cognition, memory and executive function — Singh et al. (British Journal of Sports Medicine 59(12)) — 2025 — https://bjsm.bmj.com/content/59/12/866 — study (umbrella review, 133 reviews, 2,724 RCTs) — Grade A
     Counter-case: SMD 0.42 general cognition, 0.26 memory, 0.24 executive function; larger in children and ADHD.

[S25] The effects of acute exercise on cognitive performance: a meta-analysis — Chang, Labban, Gapin, Etnier (Brain Research) — 2012 — https://www.Gwern.net/doc/iq/2012-chang.pdf — study (meta-analysis, 79 studies) — Grade A
     A single bout of exercise: g ≈ 0.10.

[S26] The effects of acute stress on core executive functions: a meta-analysis and comparison with cortisol — Shields, Sazma, Yonelinas (Neuroscience & Biobehavioral Reviews) — 2016 — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5003767 — study (meta-analysis) — Grade A
     Acute stress impairs working memory and cognitive flexibility.

## AI and thinking (2024–2026)

[S27] Generative AI without guardrails can harm learning: evidence from high school mathematics — Bastani et al. (PNAS 122(26)) — 2025 — https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=4895486 — study (field RCT, ~1,000 students) — Grade A
     With GPT access: +48% (plain) / +127% (tutor-prompted) practice performance. After removal: plain-GPT group 17% worse than no-AI control; tutor version largely avoided the harm.

[S28] How AI assistance impacts the formation of coding skills — Anthropic (Shen, Tamkin) — 2026-01-29 — https://www.anthropic.com/research/AI-assistance-coding-skills — study (RCT, n = 52, not peer-reviewed; vendor, though the finding cuts against its product) — Grade B
     AI group 50% vs 67% on a quiz about a new library (d = 0.74); speed gain not significant; delegation patterns scored worst, conceptual-question patterns scored well.

[S29] The impact of generative AI on critical thinking: self-reported reductions in cognitive effort and confidence effects — Lee, Sarkar, Tankelevitch, Drosos, Rintel, Banks, Wilson (CHI '25) — 2025 — https://advait.org/files/lee_2025_ai_critical_thinking_survey.pdf — study (survey, n = 319, 936 examples) — Grade A (self-report)
     Higher confidence in AI → less critical thinking; higher self-confidence → more. Effort shifts to verification and integration.

[S30] AI tutoring outperforms in-class active learning — Kestin, Miller, Klales, Milbourne, Ponti (Scientific Reports 15:17458) — 2025 — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12179260/ — study (crossover RCT, n = 194) — Grade A
     A pedagogy-engineered tutor (guided questioning, no answer dumping) produced >2× the learning gain in less time (median 49 vs 75 min).

[S31] Your brain on ChatGPT: accumulation of cognitive debt when using an AI assistant for essay writing — Kosmyna et al. (MIT Media Lab, arXiv 2506.08872) — 2025-06 — https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.08872v1 — preprint (n = 54; 18 in session 4) — Grade C
     LLM group: weakest EEG connectivity, low ownership, poor recall of own essays. Small, unreviewed, heavily over-reported.

[S32] Measuring the impact of early-2025 AI on experienced open-source developer productivity — METR — 2025-07 — https://simonwillison.net/2025/Jul/12/ai-open-source-productivity/ — study (RCT, 16 devs, 246 tasks; read via coverage) — Grade B
     AI-allowed tasks took 19% longer; devs predicted 24% faster and afterwards still believed ~20% faster.

[S33] We are changing our developer productivity experiment design — METR — 2026-02 — https://conffab.com/?p=10163 — post (read via coverage; metr.org unreachable) — Grade B
     The late-2025 re-run is unreliable: many devs refused to work without AI. METR believes speedups are now likely larger but its data is only weak evidence.

[S34] Endoscopist deskilling after exposure to artificial intelligence in colonoscopy — Budzyń et al. (Lancet Gastroenterology & Hepatology) — 2025 — https://wrap.warwick.ac.uk/id/eprint/191005 — study (multicentre observational, 1,443 colonoscopies) — Grade A
     Unassisted adenoma detection fell 28.4% → 22.4% in the 3 months after AI was introduced.

[S35] AI tools in society: impacts on cognitive offloading and the future of critical thinking — Gerlich (Societies 15(1)) — 2025-01 — https://doaj.org/article/d5c17dd91ce64775a065095d8af730ce — study (cross-sectional survey + interviews, n = 666) — Grade B (peer-reviewed, but correlational; graded down)
     Strong negative correlation (reported r ≈ −0.68) between AI use and critical-thinking scores, mediated by offloading. Cannot show direction.

[S36] Beware of metacognitive laziness — Fan, Tang, Le, Shen, Tan, Zhao, Shen, Li, Gašević (British Journal of Educational Technology 56(2)) — 2024 — https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.09315v1 — study (lab RCT, n = 117, 4 arms) — Grade A
     ChatGPT improved essay scores but not knowledge gain or transfer; less metacognitive activity than human-coach or checklist groups.

[S37] From chalkboards to chatbots: evaluating the impact of generative AI on learning outcomes in Nigeria — De Simone et al. (World Bank Policy Research Working Paper 11125) — 2025-05 — https://openknowledge.worldbank.org/bitstreams/cd9cca5b-2ffd-4be1-8313-0f4d2ed6ef4f/download — study (RCT, 6 weeks) — Grade A
     Teacher-guided GPT-4 tutoring: +0.31 SD overall, +0.23 SD English.

[S38] Cognitive ease at a cost: LLMs reduce mental effort but compromise depth in student scientific inquiry — Stadler, Bannert, Sailer (Computers in Human Behavior 160) — 2024 — https://opus.bibliothek.uni-augsburg.de/opus4/frontdoor/index/index/docId/114673 — study (RCT, n = 91) — Grade A
     ChatGPT vs Google: lower cognitive load, lower-quality reasoning in final recommendations.

[S39] AI makes you smarter but none the wiser: the disconnect between performance and metacognition — Fernandes et al. (Computers in Human Behavior 175) — 2026 — https://research.aalto.fi/en/publications/ai-makes-you-smarter-but-none-the-wiser-the-disconnect-between-pe/ — study (n = 246 + 452 replication) — Grade A
     AI raised LSAT-logic scores ~3 points but users overestimated by ~4; higher AI literacy → worse calibration.

## Reasoning tools

[S40] Debiasing decisions: improved decision making with a single training intervention — Morewedge et al. (Policy Insights from the Behavioral and Brain Sciences) — 2015 — https://openaccess.city.ac.uk/id/eprint/12324 — study (2 experiments) — Grade A
     One game or video cut bias ~19–32% immediately, persisting 2 months; feedback-rich game beat video.

[S41] Debiasing training transfers to improve decision making in the field — Sellier, Scopelliti, Morewedge (Psychological Science) — 2019 — https://openaccess.city.ac.uk/id/eprint/22233/ — study (n = 290, natural experiment) — Grade A
     Trained students were 29% less likely to pick the hypothesis-confirming wrong answer in an unannounced business case.

[S42] Psychological strategies for winning a geopolitical forecasting tournament — Mellers et al. (Psychological Science 25(5)) — 2014 — https://www.psychologicalscience.org/publications/observer/obsonline/predicting-uncertain-events-on-a-global-scale.html — study (randomized, 2,246 participants yr 1) — Grade A
     Training (reference classes, averaging), teaming and tracking each improved accuracy.

[S43] Developing expert political judgment: the impact of training and practice on judgmental accuracy — Chang, Chen, Mellers, Tetlock (Judgment and Decision Making 11(5)) — 2016 — https://dlab.sauder.ubc.ca/sjdm/journal/16/16511/jdm16511.html — study (4-year tournament) — Grade A
     Under one hour of probabilistic-reasoning training improved Brier scores 6–11%.

[S44] Evaluating the effectiveness of the PreMortem technique on plan confidence — Veinott, Klein, Wiggins (ISCRAM proceedings) — 2010 — https://core.ac.uk/works/49608374 — study (n = 178, conference paper) — Grade B
     Premortem reduced overconfidence in a plan more than critique, pros/cons, or cons-only.

[S45] Learning through case comparisons: a meta-analytic review — Alfieri, Nokes-Malach, Schunn (Educational Psychologist 48(2)) — 2013 — https://www.lrdc.pitt.edu/Schunn/papers/ContrastingCasesMeta-AlfieriEtAl2013.pdf — study (57 experiments) — Grade A
     Comparing cases side by side beats traditional instruction, d = 0.50.

[S46] Judgmental decomposition: when does it work? — MacGregor, Armstrong (International Journal of Forecasting 10(4)) — 1994 — https://marketing.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2022/02/140-JSA-Judgmental-Decomposition-When-Does-It-Work.pdf — study (280 subjects, 1,078 estimates) — Grade A
     Fermi-style decomposition improves estimates only for extreme, uncertain quantities.

[S47] Why don't well-educated adults understand accumulation? — Cronin, Gonzalez, Sterman (OBHDP 108(1)) — 2009 — https://mitmgmtfaculty.mit.edu/jsterman/whydontwelleducatedadultsunderstand/ — study — Grade A
     Educated adults fail simple stock-and-flow tasks via a "correlation heuristic"; not explained by graph literacy or motivation.

[S48] Learning styles: concepts and evidence — Pashler, McDaniel, Rohrer, Bjork (Psychological Science in the Public Interest) — 2008 — https://digitalcommons.usf.edu/psy_facpub/1765/ — study (review) — Grade A
     No adequate evidence for matching instruction to "learning styles"; the few valid tests contradict it.

[S49] The generation effect: a meta-analytic review — Bertsch, Pesta, Wiscott, McDaniel (Memory & Cognition) — 2007 — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6099118 — study (meta-analysis, 86 studies; read via citing review) — Grade A
     Generating an answer beats reading it, effect ≈ 0.40.

[S50] Not new, but nearly forgotten: the testing effect decreases or even disappears as the complexity of learning materials increases — van Gog, Sweller (Educational Psychology Review 27) — 2015 — https://pure.eur.nl/en/publications/not-new-but-nearly-forgotten-the-testing-effect-decreases-or-even/ — study (review) — Grade A
     Counter-case: retrieval practice is mostly tested on simple material; weaker for high element-interactivity content.

[S51] The testing effect is alive and well with complex materials — Karpicke, Aue (Educational Psychology Review 27) — 2015 — https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10648-015-9310-x — study (reply) — Grade A
     Rebuttal: "complexity" was undefined and studies showing effects on complex material were omitted.
