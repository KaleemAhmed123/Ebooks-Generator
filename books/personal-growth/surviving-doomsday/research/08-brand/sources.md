## Does public work turn into jobs, clients, money?

[S1] Big Data and Bigger Firms: A Labor Market Channel — Abhinav Gupta, Naman Nishesh, Elena Simintzi — working paper, presented AEA 2025 / NBER — https://conference.nber.org/conf_papers/f210039/f210039.pdf — study (quasi-experiment) — Grade B
     GitHub's 19 May 2016 change let users show private contributions; afterwards, +1 SD of visible contributions → 5.7% more moves to large firms, mostly out of small firms. Visibility of existing work, not new work, moved people. Not yet peer-reviewed.

[S2] Career Concerns as Public Good: The Role of Signaling for Open Source Software Development — ifo Institute working paper — 2024 — https://www.ifo.de/publikationen/2024/working-paper/career-concerns-public-good-role-signaling-open-source-software-0 — study — Grade B
     ~22,900 GitHub developers: OSS activity rises ~16% during a job-search window (job relocators vs other relocators), skewed toward visible, market-valued work. Developers themselves behave as if visibility pays.

[S3] Economic returns to open source participation (Apache panel) — Il-Horn Hann, Jeff Roberts, Sandra Slaughter, Roy Fielding — ICIS 2002; extended later — https://flosshub.org/node/104 — peer-reviewed study — Grade A (old data)
     Four-year Apache panel: raw contribution volume did not raise wages; reaching higher merit rank did, by 13–27%. A credible third-party-verified signal paid, effort alone did not.

[S4] Activity traces and signals in software developer recruitment and hiring — Jennifer Marlow, Laura Dabbish — CSCW 2013 — https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~xia/resources/Documents/Marlow-cscw13.pdf — qualitative interview study — Grade A (qualitative, pre-AI)
     Employers read GitHub traces as more reliable than résumés because they are hard to fake; strongest signals were sustained history and contribution to high-status projects.

[S5] A causal test of the strength of weak ties — Karthik Rajkumar, Guillaume Saint-Jacques, Iavor Bojinov, Erik Brynjolfsson, Sinan Aral — Science 377(6612), Sept 2022 — https://digitaleconomy.stanford.edu/publication/a-causal-test-of-the-strength-of-weak-ties — randomized experiments, 20M+ LinkedIn users, 5 years — Grade A
     Moderately weak ties produce the most job mobility (inverted U); weak ties matter more in digital industries, strong ties in less digital ones.

[S6] Dormant Ties: The Value of Reconnecting — Daniel Z. Levin, Jorge Walter, J. Keith Murnighan — Organization Science 22(4), 2011 — https://ideas.repec.org/a/inm/ororsc/v22y2011i4p923-939.html — field study (Executive MBAs) — Grade A
     Reconnected dormant ties gave more valuable help than current ties: trust residue of strong ties plus novelty of weak ones, and quicker to approach than strangers.

[S7] Independent workers' sources of assignments (State of Independence survey for MBO Partners), reported in HBR "The Freelance Economy Still Runs on Word of Mouth" — MBO Partners / Emergent Research — survey year not confirmed (c. mid-2010s) — https://tagteam.harvard.edu/hub_feeds/924/feed_items/1973079 — survey — Grade B (dated)
     Most assignments come by word of mouth; social media named among top-3 sources by 18%, marketplaces 10%; for those earning >$100k, 1% and 6%.

[S8] Buying behaviour of professional-services buyers (1,028 buyers; "Beyond Referrals" report) — Hinge Research Institute — 2014 — https://kevin.lexblog.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/111/2014/02/HIN-BeyondReferrals-Report-021314-1.pdf — survey — Grade C (marketing firm selling the conclusion)
     81% of buyers check the provider's website, 63% search them online, 62% ask colleagues; 3.2 checks on average. Even referred buyers verify online.

[S9] The 95:5 rule (How B2B brands grow) — John Dawes, Ehrenberg-Bass Institute, for the LinkedIn B2B Institute — 2021 — https://www.marketingweek.com/peter-weinberg-jon-lombardo-95-5-rule — research report + reporting — Grade B (LinkedIn-funded)
     Firms switch major service providers about every five years, so ~5% of buyers are in-market in a quarter; buyers enter the market on their own timetable.

[S10] 2024 B2B Thought Leadership Impact Report — Edelman & LinkedIn — 2024 — https://www.edelman.com/expertise/business-marketing/2024-b2b-thought-leadership-report — survey, ~3,500 managers, 7 countries incl. India — Grade C (both firms sell this)
     73% of decision-makers say thought-leadership content is a more trustworthy basis for judging capability than marketing materials.

[S11] Gartner B2B buying-journey research — Gartner — figures circulated 2019 onward — https://www.growthmethod.com/gartner-b2b-buying-journey/ (secondary summary) — survey — Grade B
     B2B buyers spend ~17% of buying time meeting suppliers, split across the shortlist; most of the journey is self-directed research.

[S12] AI-related work on Upwork, Q1 2026 results — Upwork (via Staffing Industry Analysts) — 2026 — https://staffingindustry.com/news/global-daily-news/upwork-reports-q1-revenue-rises-14-layoffs-ahead — company earnings — Grade B (incentive to talk up AI)
     AI-related gross services volume >$300M in Q1 2026, +40% y/y; "AI integration and automation", the largest AI sub-category, +50%.

## The AI flood: what cuts through

[S13] AI-generated share of new web articles (Common Crawl sample, 65k URLs, Jan 2020–May 2025) — Graphite, reported by Axios — 2025-10-14 — https://axios.com/2025/10/14/ai-generated-writing-humans — data analysis with a detector — Grade B (detector error unknown)
     AI-written share passed ~50% in Nov 2024 and plateaued (51.7% May 2025); 86% of top-ranking Google pages were still human-written.

[S14] Over half of long LinkedIn posts likely AI-generated — Originality.ai — 2024-11 — https://originality.ai/pt/blog/ai-content-published-linkedin — vendor analysis, 8,795 posts — Grade C (sells the detector)
     54% of 100+-word posts flagged likely-AI by Oct 2024; flagged posts got 45% less engagement.

[S15] Google Search's guidance about AI-generated content — Google Search Central — 2023-02-08 — https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2023/02/google-search-and-ai-content — official platform docs — Grade A (for Google's stated policy)
     Ranking rewards original, people-first content showing E-E-A-T (experience first); AI use is neither banned nor favoured; mass-produced pages without added value fall under spam policy.

[S16] Google users are less likely to click on links when an AI summary appears — Pew Research Center — 2025-07-22 — https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2025/07/22/google-user — browsing data, 900 US adults, 68,879 searches — Grade A
     Clicks on results fell to 8% with an AI Overview vs 15% without; 1% clicked a source inside the overview.

[S17] 96.55% of content gets no traffic from Google — Ahrefs (Tim Soulo) — 2023 — https://ahrefs.com/blog/search-traffic-study/ — vendor data, ~14B pages — Grade B
     Almost no published page gets search traffic. Pre-AI-Overview baseline.

[S18] Discriminating deceptive from truthful statements using the verifiability approach: a meta-analysis — UvA / Maastricht repository (authors not confirmed in this pass) — https://dare.uva.nl/id/3f59b558-22fd-428f-9a97-43135f477be5 — meta-analysis — Grade A (for its domain)
     Truthful accounts carry more checkable details (named people, recordings); used here only by analogy — see report.

## How the reluctance to post holds up against evidence

[S19] The spotlight effect in social judgment — Thomas Gilovich, Victoria Medvec, Kenneth Savitsky — JPSP, 2000 — https://www.kellogg.northwestern.edu/academics-research/research/detail/2000/the-spotlight-effect-in-social-judgment-an-egocentric-bias/ — experiments — Grade A
     People estimate about twice as many observers noticed them as actually did.

[S20] Is it as bad as we fear? Overestimating the extremity of others' judgments — Kenneth Savitsky, Nicholas Epley, Thomas Gilovich — JPSP, 2001 — https://www.williams.edu/profile/ksavitsk/ — experiments — Grade A
     After a public mishap, people expect harsher judgments than observers actually make.

[S21] Underestimating how much others appreciate being reached out to — Peggy Liu, SoYon Rim, Kate Min — JPSP, 2022 — https://business.ku.edu/people-underestimate-surprising-impact-reaching-out-study-finds — 13 preregistered experiments, ~6,000 people — Grade A
     Initiators systematically underestimate recipients' appreciation, more so when the contact is unexpected.

[S22] Humblebragging: a distinct — and ineffective — self-presentation strategy — Ovul Sezer, Francesca Gino, Michael Norton — JPSP, 2018 — https://dash.harvard.edu/entities/publication/73120379-03a9-6bd4-e053-0100007fdf3b — 9 studies incl. field experiment — Grade A
     Humblebrags lower liking and perceived competence more than plain bragging; seen as insincere.

[S23] Exposure and affect: overview and meta-analysis 1968–1987 — Robert Bornstein — Psychological Bulletin, 1989 — https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10198302 (cites it) — meta-analysis, 208 experiments — Grade A (lab, old)
     Repeated exposure raises liking, r ≈ 0.26; stronger with a delay between exposure and judgment.

[S24] Write shorter messages — Todd Rogers, Jessica Lasky-Fink (HKS) — https://www.hks.harvard.edu/sites/default/files/HKSEE/HKSEE%20PDFs/Write%20shorter%20messages.pdf — field experiment, 7,002 recipients — Grade B (summary of peer-reviewed work)
     A 49-word email nearly doubled responses vs a 127-word version.

## Platforms, ownership, risks

[S25] LinkedIn feed: LLM-based retrieval + Generative Recommender ranking — LinkedIn Engineering blog (2026-03-12) and arXiv 2510.14223 "Large Scale Retrieval for the LinkedIn Feed using Causal Language Models" — https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2510.14223 — official engineering docs — Grade A (for how the system is built; says nothing about "hacks")
     Out-of-network posts are retrieved by topical-interest embeddings (2,000 candidates from hundreds of millions); ranking models a member's 1,000+ past interactions as a sequence.

[S26] Musk on X link reach — reported by Business Insider / GIGAZINE — 2024-11-24 — https://wbgsv0a.gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20241126-twitter-x-link-post-throttles — owner statement — Grade B
     Musk advised writing the description in the post and putting the link in a reply; link posts get less reach.

[S27] X open-sources its recommendation algorithm — TechCrunch — 2026-01-20 — https://techcrunch.com/2026/01/20/x-open-sources-its-algorithm-while-facing-a-transparency-fine-and-grok-controversies — reporting — Grade B
     X's For You code is public; claims about specific weightings come from third-party readers of it (C).

[S28] Facebook Zero: considering life after the demise of organic reach — Social@Ogilvy — 2014-03 — https://netimperative.com/2014/03/11/facebook-roi-or-rip-brands-organic-reach-is-destined-to-die-out — agency analysis, 106 pages — Grade B
     Organic reach of brand pages fell from ~16% (2012) to ~6% (Feb 2014), ~2% for large pages. The canonical case of a rented audience being repriced.

[S29] Creator burnout surveys — Billion Dollar Boy (Censuswide) 2025 52%; Creators 4 Mental Health 2025 62% — https://www.billiondollarboy.com/news/over-half-of-creators-face-burnout — vendor surveys — Grade C
     Full-time creators report high burnout. Population is professional creators, not engineers who post part-time.

[S30] #1 on HN for six hours: postmortem — Kevin Burke — https://kevin.burke.dev/kevin/hn-popular-post-stats/ — practitioner post — Grade C (anecdote)
     A #1 HN post drew ~31.8k pageviews; one data point showing community spikes are large and short.

[S31] Exploratory interviews with 15 recruiters (Brazil, Canada, US) on using GitHub data to assess hard skills — Johnatan Oliveira, Eduardo Figueiredo (UFMG) — 2024 preprint — https://homepages.dcc.ufmg.br/~figueiredo/publications/latam2024preprint.pdf — qualitative study — Grade B
     The only AI-era recruiter study found; search summaries gave its scope but no usable findings. Listed as a gap, not as evidence.
