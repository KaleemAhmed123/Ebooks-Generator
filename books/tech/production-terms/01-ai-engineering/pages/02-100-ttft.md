## TTFT

*time to first token*

How long before the first token appears. Dominated by prefill — processing the
input — so it scales with prompt length.

A 400ms TTFT feels instant in a streaming interface. A 4s TTFT feels broken even
if total generation time is identical, because the user stares at nothing.

### How it works

Time to first token is how long the user waits in front of an empty space before
text starts arriving. It breaks into three parts:

**Queueing** — waiting for capacity.
**Prefill** — the model reading your entire prompt.
**First decode** — producing the opening token.

Prefill usually dominates, and it scales with prompt length. **This is the
counterintuitive part: a longer prompt makes the *start* slower**, quite
separately from making the answer longer. Doubling your retrieved context can
double the silence before anything appears.

It matters more than total time because of how waiting is experienced. Text
arriving steadily reads as fast even across twelve seconds. A blank screen for
three seconds reads as broken, even when the full answer then arrives quickly.

### In practice

This is why **trimming retrieved context often improves the feel of a product
more than switching to a faster model does.**

Five well-reranked chunks instead of forty cuts prefill dramatically, and users
experience that directly as responsiveness.

If TTFT is your problem, look at prompt size before you look at anything else.
It is almost always the answer, and it is usually the cheapest thing on the list
to change.
