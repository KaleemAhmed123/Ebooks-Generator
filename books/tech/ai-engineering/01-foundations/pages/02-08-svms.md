## Support vector machines

- A **support vector machine (SVM)** separates two classes by drawing the boundary with the **widest possible gap** between them.
- Of the infinitely many lines that split the data, it picks the one with the largest **margin** — the biggest buffer to the nearest points of either class.
- Those nearest points are the **support vectors**. They alone define the boundary; move any other point and nothing changes.

### The kernel trick

- When classes are not separable by a straight line, SVMs use a **kernel** — a way to measure similarity as if the data had been lifted into a higher dimension where a flat boundary *does* separate them, without ever computing those coordinates.
- This lets a linear method carve curved boundaries cheaply.

:::note
Before deep learning, kernel SVMs were the state of the art for many tasks. Today they shine on small-to-medium datasets with clear margins, where they are fast and need little data — a real advantage when labelled examples are scarce.
:::
