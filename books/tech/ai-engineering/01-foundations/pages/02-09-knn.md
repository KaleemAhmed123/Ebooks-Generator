## k-nearest neighbours

- **k-nearest neighbours (k-NN)** is the simplest classifier there is: to label a new point, find the `k` closest known points and take their majority vote.
- There is no training. The model *is* the dataset. All the work happens at prediction time.

### The choices that decide everything

- **k** — how many neighbours vote. Small `k` is jumpy and noise-sensitive; large `k` blurs the boundary. Odd values avoid ties.
- **Distance** — usually Euclidean (straight-line), or cosine for embeddings. This is the norm from Module 1 doing real work.

:::warn
k-NN degrades in high dimensions (the curse of dimensionality) and is slow at scale — every prediction compares against every stored point. Yet its core idea, "find the nearest vectors," is exactly what a **vector database** does for retrieval-augmented generation. Old idea, central to modern AI.
:::
