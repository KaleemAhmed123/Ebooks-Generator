## Singular value decomposition

:::note
**Optional deep-dive — safe to skip on a first read.** Come back when you meet LoRA or low-rank methods later and want the idea underneath them.
:::

- **Singular value decomposition (SVD)** breaks any matrix into three simpler ones: `A = U Σ Vᵀ`.
- Read it as: every transformation is a **rotation**, then a **stretch** along axes, then another **rotation**. `U` and `Vᵀ` rotate; `Σ` (a diagonal matrix of **singular values**) does the stretching.
- It is the most general matrix decomposition — it works on any matrix, not just square ones, which is why it appears everywhere.

### Why it earns its place

- The singular values are ranked. The largest capture the matrix's dominant structure; the smallest are near-noise.
- Keep the top `k` and discard the rest, and you get the best possible rank-`k` approximation of the matrix — the mathematical core of compression and **low-rank** methods.

:::note
LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation) — the standard way to fine-tune large language models cheaply, as of 2026 — rests on this idea: the weight *change* during fine-tuning is nearly low-rank, so it can be stored as a tiny pair of thin matrices instead of a full one.
:::
