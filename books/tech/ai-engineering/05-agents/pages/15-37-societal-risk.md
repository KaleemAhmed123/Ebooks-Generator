## Societal risk: a bridge

- Beyond any single agent's safety lies the *aggregate* impact of autonomous agents on society — economic, informational, and structural. This is the domain of Booklet 6 (Production Safety & Society); here is the bridge, because an agent engineer's choices roll up into these effects.

- **Labor.** As task horizons grow (15-34), agents automate not just tasks but jobs. The question shifts from "can an agent do this?" to "what happens to the people who did it?" — a transition to manage, not a fact to celebrate or fear.
- **Information ecosystem.** Autonomous agents can generate and act on content at scale — useful (research, support) and harmful (spam, manipulation, synthetic personas at scale). The web fills with agent-generated and agent-consumed content, changing what "information" means.
- **Security.** Autonomous cyber-capable agents cut both ways — better defense (automated patching, monitoring) and cheaper, scaled offense (the dangerous-capability category, 15-33). Agent-vs-agent security is an emerging reality.
- **Concentration.** The most capable agents require frontier models, compute, and infrastructure concentrated in a few organizations — raising questions of access, dependence, and power that no single engineer controls but every one contributes to.

:::note
This page is a deliberate hand-off, not a conclusion. Booklet 5 taught you to *build* agents — perceive, use tools, reason, remember, orchestrate, and make autonomous — and to make them *safe at the system level*. Booklet 6 takes the next altitude: deploying at scale, and the responsibility that comes with technology this powerful. The engineer who can build an autonomous agent *and* reason about its societal footprint is the one these roles actually need — capability paired with judgment, which is the through-line of this entire series.
:::
