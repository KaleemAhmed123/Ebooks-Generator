## STaR: bootstrapping reasoning

- **STaR** (Self-Taught Reasoner, Zelikman et al., 2022) is the most bounded and foundational self-improvement method: a model **teaches itself to reason better** by generating its own training data from problems it can already solve. **[VERIFY]**

- **The loop:** for each problem, the model generates a **rationale** (chain-of-thought) and an answer. Keep the rationales that reached the **correct** answer (the answer key is the evaluator). Fine-tune the model on those good rationales. The improved model solves more problems, generating more good rationales, and the cycle repeats — the model bootstraps its own reasoning ability.
- **The clever bit — rationalization:** for problems it gets *wrong*, STaR gives the model the correct answer and asks it to produce a rationale that leads there (working backward), then trains on those too — so it learns from hard problems, not just easy ones.
- **Why it matters here:** STaR is the seed of the "reasoning model" era (Booklet 4's RL-for-reasoning, DeepSeek-R1). Its core idea — *generate reasoning, keep what verifiably works, train on it* — is exactly how modern reasoning models are trained at scale. It is self-improvement with the answer key as the ground-truth evaluator.

:::interview
**"How can a model improve its own reasoning without new human data?"** STaR's bootstrap. It generates chain-of-thought rationales for problems, keeps the ones that reach the *verifiably correct* answer (the answer key is the evaluator), and fine-tunes on them — for problems it fails, it rationalizes backward from the given answer and trains on that too. The better model then produces more good rationales, and the loop compounds. It works because correctness is checkable; it's the direct ancestor of today's RL-trained reasoning models. No new human-written reasoning is needed — just problems with checkable answers.
:::
