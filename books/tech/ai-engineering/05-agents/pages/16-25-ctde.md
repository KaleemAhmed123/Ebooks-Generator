## Centralized training, decentralized execution

- The key idea that makes MARL tractable is **CTDE — Centralized Training, Decentralized Execution.** Train the agents together with access to global information, but let each act on its *own* local view at run time. It is the framework nearly all modern MARL algorithms use. **[VERIFY]**

- **The trick:** during **training**, a **centralized critic** (the value function, Booklet 4) can see *everyone's* observations and actions — the global state. This tames non-stationarity (the critic accounts for what all agents do) and credit assignment (it can attribute outcomes with full information). But each agent's **policy** — the thing that picks actions — depends only on that agent's *own* local observation.
- **Why decentralized execution matters:** at run time there is often *no* central coordinator — each robot, drone, or player must act on what *it* alone can see, in real time, possibly without communication. CTDE gives you agents that were *trained* with the benefit of global information but can *act* independently. You get coordinated behavior without needing a central brain at deployment.
- **The intuition:** it is like a coach who, during practice, sees the whole field and teaches each player how to play their position; in the game, each player acts on their own view, but plays well *because* they were coached with the full picture. Training-time omniscience, execution-time locality.

:::interview
"What is CTDE and what problems does it solve?"

Centralized Training, Decentralized Execution. During training, a centralized critic has access to all agents' observations and actions (the global state), which tames MARL's two hard problems — non-stationarity (it accounts for everyone learning at once) and credit assignment (it can attribute a shared reward with full information). But each agent's *policy* uses only its own local observation, so at execution the agents act independently with no central controller — essential for robots/drones/players that must act in real time on partial views without communication. The analogy: a coach who sees the whole field in practice teaches each player, who then plays their own position in the game.
:::
