# Glossary

## How to read it

- 875 terms, alphabetical. Every one of them is defined somewhere in the six booklets, and every entry here is one line
- The last column says **which booklet introduces the term**, so one line is never the end of the answer

### The pointer

`B4` means the term is introduced in **Booklet 4**. `B1 · B2` means two booklets use it; it is defined here where it is first taught. Punctuation and case are ignored in the ordering, so `K-means` files under K and `p50` under P.

| # | Booklet | What it owns |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Foundations | math, probability, optimization, classical ML |
| 2 | Deep Learning | networks, training, vision, speech |
| 3 | Language | NLP, embeddings, the transformer |
| 4 | LLMs | RL, RLHF, training from scratch, LLM engineering, RAG |
| 5 | Agents | tools, protocols (MCP), agent frameworks, multi-agent |
| 6 | Production | serving, infrastructure, safety, system design |

- **One term is defined once.** Where a later booklet uses an idea an earlier one owns, it points rather than repeats — so the pointer here is the booklet that explains it, not the last one that mentioned it
- A **bold term** inside a definition has an entry of its own
