# About the author

**Kaleem Ahmed** is a software engineer. He works mostly in Node.js, TypeScript
and Python, on the kind of systems that have to keep running long after the
demo: integrations, data synchronisation, document automation, real-time
features, AI-backed products, and the infrastructure underneath all of it.

Most of what is in these pages was learned the ordinary way. A model that
worked in a notebook and fell over in production. A RAG pipeline that retrieved
confidently and answered wrong. An agent that looped, or took an action nobody
had thought to forbid. Each failure turned out to need a layer he had been
content to ignore until then.

He writes things down because writing is where he finds out whether he actually
understood something. An explanation that reads cleanly usually means the idea
is settled; one that keeps needing another clause usually means it is not. Much
of this volume is the second kind, rewritten until it became the first.

His aim when explaining is narrow: remove the unnecessary complexity without
removing the necessary complexity. A good explanation should make a hard thing
approachable while being honest that the hard part is still there.

He is not certain about all of it. This field moves faster than any book can,
and some pages describe choices he would already make differently. The reasoning
is written down precisely so it can be argued with rather than copied. If
something here is wrong, out of date, or badly explained, he would rather hear
about it than not.
