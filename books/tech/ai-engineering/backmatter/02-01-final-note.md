# A final note

## You got to the end

- That is more than 900 pages across six booklets, from the first vector to a production serving stack. Whether you read it in order or opened one the week you had to build the thing, thank you for the time

### What to do next

- **Build one thing end to end.** Pick a flagship from Booklet 6 — a GPT from scratch, a RAG service, a coding agent — and work it on your own machine until it runs. Reading the mechanism and assembling it under your own hands are different skills
- **Name one number you inherited.** A temperature, a chunk size, a top-k, a timeout, a context budget. Most bad AI systems in these pages started as a default nobody had a reason for
- **Defend one page out loud.** Take a topic you expect to be asked — attention, DPO, KV-cache, the lethal trifecta — and explain it against a clock, to a wall if you have to. Knowing it and being able to say it are not the same

### Where this will be wrong

- **The frontier moves fastest here.** Every version number, benchmark figure and framework API was checked against a primary source as this edition was built. Some of it is already stale, and the pages tell you which claims are load-bearing enough to re-check
- **Some of it is a judgement call.** When to fine-tune versus retrieve, when an agent beats a workflow, when to self-host versus call an API. The reasoning is written down so you can disagree with the reasoning rather than the conclusion
- **Some subjects are compressed on purpose.** The deepest math and the newest papers get a page each, not a chapter. Where a topic needs more than this volume gives it, the page says so and points you at the source
