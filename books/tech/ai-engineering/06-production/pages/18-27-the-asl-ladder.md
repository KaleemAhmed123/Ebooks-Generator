## The ASL ladder

- Anthropic's **AI Safety Levels** make the tiered-capability idea concrete, modelled on biosafety levels (BSL-1 through 4 for pathogen labs). Each level names a capability regime and the safeguards it requires. **[VERIFY current tiers]**

- **The rungs.** ASL-2 is today's frontier baseline with standard safeguards. **ASL-3** (activated May 2025) marks capabilities that meaningfully uplift CBRN misuse, triggering hardened security and deployment controls. ASL-4 and above concern models that can *automate AI research itself* — the point at which capability could compound.
- **The trigger is capability, not calendar.** A model is evaluated against the thresholds; crossing one *activates* the corresponding safeguards regardless of the release schedule. Once the AI-R&D threshold is crossed, an *affirmative safety case* (next page) is required before deployment — the lab must argue, not assume, the model is safe.

:::note
The biosafety-level analogy is the useful mental model: you do not handle a level-3 pathogen with level-1 precautions, and you do not deploy a CBRN-capable model with baseline safeguards. The ladder turns "is this model dangerous?" from a vibe into a *procedure* — run the dangerous-capability evals, see which rung the result lands on, apply that rung's required controls. The interview point is that the tier drives the safeguards, so capability evaluation (18-30) is the load-bearing step.
:::
