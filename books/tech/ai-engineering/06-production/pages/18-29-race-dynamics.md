## Race dynamics and competitor-adjustment

- Voluntary safety frameworks have a game-theory problem: if safeguards slow you down and a competitor ships without them, the safety-conscious lab loses the market. All three 2025+ frameworks answer this with a **competitor-adjustment clause** — permission to relax requirements if a peer lab deploys comparable capability without comparable safeguards. **[VERIFY]**

- **The debate.** *Critics:* if every lab will lower its bar when a competitor defects, the equilibrium drifts toward defection — a race to the bottom dressed as pragmatism. *Defenders:* unilateral safeguards just hand the frontier to a *less* safety-conscious lab, so a conditional clause is the realistic second-best. Both positions hinge on an assumption about whether the defecting lab is safer or less safe than you.
- **This is why regulation exists.** Voluntary frameworks cannot escape the race dynamic on their own — only an external, binding floor can. Government bodies (UK AISI, US CAISI, EU AI Office) are the emerging counterpart, turning voluntary commitments into enforceable ones (18-35).

:::note
The competitor-adjustment clause is the honest seam in self-governance: it admits the frameworks are only as strong as the competitive pressure allows. For an engineer, the takeaway is that *voluntary* safety commitments are structurally fragile — they hold in calm markets and bend under race pressure — which is the whole argument for binding regulation as the backstop. Understanding this tension, rather than treating the frameworks as settled guarantees, is what a thoughtful answer conveys.
:::
