## DPO

- **Direct preference optimization (DPO)** (Rafailov et al., 2023) gets the result of RLHF without the reward model or the RL loop. Its insight: the optimal RLHF policy has a **closed-form** relationship to the reward, so you can skip straight to optimising the policy on preference pairs.
- One loss, trained like ordinary supervised learning:

:::mint
```
L = − log σ( β · [ logπ(chosen) − logπ_ref(chosen) ]
              − β · [ logπ(rejected) − logπ_ref(rejected) ] )
```
Raise the policy's log-prob on **chosen**, lower it on **rejected** — each measured against a frozen **reference** model (the SFT model). β sets the strength.
:::

- The reference model is the KL leash in disguise: the `− logπ_ref` terms penalise drifting from the SFT model, exactly what RLHF's explicit KL penalty did — but for free, inside the loss.

- The payoff: no reward model to train, no PPO to tune, half the memory, and a stable run. DPO aligned Zephyr, and variants train Llama, Qwen, and most open instruct models today.

:::warn
DPO is **offline** — it only ever sees your fixed pair dataset, so it cannot explore new answers the way PPO can, and it is sensitive to the quality and coverage of those pairs. It can also **over-optimise**: pushing chosen up and rejected down can drag *both* probabilities down, making the model oddly terse. The successors on the next page each patch one of these gaps.
:::
