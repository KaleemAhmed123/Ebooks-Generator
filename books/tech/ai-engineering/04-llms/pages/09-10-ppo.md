## PPO

- **Proximal policy optimization (PPO)** is the default policy-gradient algorithm — stable, simple to tune, and the workhorse behind RLHF. It is actor-critic with one guard rail added.
- The problem it solves: a big policy-gradient step can **destroy** the policy. You update on data from the *old* policy, but the update changes the policy a lot, so that data no longer describes what the new policy does. PPO forbids large steps.

:::mint
```
ratio  = π_new(a|s) / π_old(a|s)          # how much the policy changed
L = min( ratio · A ,  clip(ratio, 1−ε, 1+ε) · A )
```
The clip caps the ratio near 1 (ε≈0.2). Beyond that, more change earns no more reward — so the update stays "proximal" (close) to the old policy.
:::

- **GRPO** (group relative policy optimization, DeepSeek, 2024) is PPO's fast-rising successor for LLMs. It **drops the critic**: sample a *group* of answers to one prompt, use the group's average reward as the baseline, and compute each answer's advantage relative to that. No value network means ~40% less memory and fewer moving parts — it trained DeepSeek-R1 (Jan 2025) and Qwen3.

:::warn
PPO's stability is bought with sensitivity. The clip ε, the KL penalty, the learning rate, and the reward scale all interact; a small mis-set and reward either flatlines or the model **collapses** into gibberish that games the reward. RLHF's reputation for being finicky is mostly PPO tuning.
:::
