## "A stakeholder wants to 'add an AI chatbot' to everything. How do you respond?"

- **What they're screening for:** product judgment and the ability to push back constructively on hype-driven requests without being dismissive.
- **A strong answer shows:**
  - **Engage with the underlying goal** — "what problem are we solving for users?" A chatbot is a solution looking for a problem until you find the job-to-be-done.
  - **Redirect to value** — identify where conversational AI genuinely helps (ambiguous support, discovery) vs where a button, form, or search is faster and better.
  - **Name the costs** — latency, $, hallucination risk, maintenance, and that a bad AI feature erodes trust.
  - **Propose a test** — a narrow pilot on one high-value use case with a success metric, before a platform-wide rollout.
- Show you can say "here's a better way to get what you want" rather than a flat yes or no.

:::warn
Weak: "Sure, I'll add a chatbot everywhere" (no judgment) or "That's a bad idea" (dismissive). Strong: reframe to the user problem, pilot the one place it adds value, measure, then decide.
:::

:::interview
What's really being tested: product sense and constructive pushback — mapping a hype request back to a real user problem and a measurable pilot.
:::
