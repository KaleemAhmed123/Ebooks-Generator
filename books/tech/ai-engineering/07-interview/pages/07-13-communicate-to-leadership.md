## "How do you communicate AI capabilities and limitations to leadership?"

- **What they're screening for:** translating technical reality into business terms, and resisting pressure to over-promise.
- **A strong answer shows:**
  - **Lead with outcomes and tradeoffs** — "we can do X at Y quality for Z cost; here's what we can't do reliably yet," framed in terms of user/business impact, not jargon.
  - **Set honest expectations** — be concrete about accuracy, failure modes, and timeline uncertainty; under-promise rather than fuel hype that sets the team up to fail.
  - **Give options, not absolutes** — "a fast/cheap version does 80% now; the last 20% costs 3× — which do you want?"
  - **Separate hype from reality** — gently correct headline-driven expectations with what's actually feasible for *our* data and constraints.
- The skill is being the **honest translator** between what executives read and what the team can deliver.

:::warn
Weak: "I told them it would be amazing and transform everything." Strong: outcome-framed, honest about limits and uncertainty, offering cost/quality options — building trust over hype.
:::

:::interview
What's really being tested: that you translate capability/limitation into business terms honestly, manage hype, and present tradeoffs as choices — earning trust, not selling.
:::
