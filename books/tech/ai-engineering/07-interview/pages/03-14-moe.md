## What is a Mixture-of-Experts layer, and what does it buy you?

- In a **Mixture-of-Experts (MoE)** transformer, the feed-forward block is replaced by many parallel "expert" FFNs plus a small **router** that sends each token to only a few experts (e.g. 2 of 8).
- The win is **decoupling parameters from compute**: total parameters (and thus knowledge capacity) grow with the expert count, but each token only activates a couple of experts, so compute per token stays low.
- So a model can have, say, hundreds of billions of *total* parameters but only tens of billions *active* per token — the quality of a huge model at the inference cost of a small one.
- Costs: all experts must sit in memory (big VRAM footprint), routing can **load-imbalance** (some experts overused), and training needs auxiliary losses to spread tokens evenly.

:::interview
What's really being tested:

the total-vs-active parameter distinction (capacity up, compute flat) and the real operational costs — memory for all experts and load-balancing the router.
:::
