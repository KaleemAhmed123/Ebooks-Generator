## What does PCA do, and when would you reach for it?

- **PCA (principal component analysis)** finds the directions along which the data varies most, and re-expresses each point using just those directions. You keep the top few and drop the rest.
- Mechanically: centre the data, compute the covariance matrix, take its top eigenvectors (the **principal components**). Projecting onto the top k gives the best k-dimensional linear summary — the one that loses the least variance.
- **Use it for:** compressing features before a classical model, de-noising, visualising high-dimensional data in 2-D/3-D, and killing multicollinearity (correlated features) before linear regression.
- **Don't use it when:** you need interpretable features (components are blends of originals), the structure is non-linear (use UMAP/t-SNE or an autoencoder), or variance ≠ importance for your task.

:::warn
PCA is unsupervised — it maximises variance, not class separation. A direction with huge variance can be irrelevant noise. If you need the split, LDA or a supervised model may beat it.
:::

:::interview
What's really being tested:

that you see PCA as "best linear low-rank summary by variance," know it's unsupervised, and know its linear assumption is the thing that breaks.
:::
