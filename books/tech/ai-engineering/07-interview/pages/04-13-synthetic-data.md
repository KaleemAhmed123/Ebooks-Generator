## When is synthetic (model-generated) training data useful, and what is model collapse?

- **Synthetic data** — training examples generated by an LLM — is now central: distillation from a stronger model, instruction/preference data, and verifiable reasoning traces. It's cheap, scalable, and avoids some privacy issues.
- When it works: a **strong teacher** generating data for a weaker student, or any case where a **verifier filters** the output (keep only correct solutions). Quality control is the whole game.
- **Model collapse:** if you train models on their own (or other models') unfiltered outputs across generations, the distribution narrows — rare events vanish, diversity decays, and quality degrades generation over generation. The model forgets the tails of the real distribution.
- Guard: keep a **core of real human data**, filter synthetic data hard (verifiers, dedup, quality scoring), and don't train on un-curated model output at scale. [VERIFY: current model-collapse findings.]
