## How does maximum likelihood connect cross-entropy and MSE?

- **Maximum likelihood estimation (MLE)** picks the parameters that make the observed data most probable: maximise `P(data | θ)`. Taking the log turns the product over examples into a sum, and maximising log-likelihood = minimising *negative* log-likelihood.
- Assume the target is **categorical** (a class) → negative log-likelihood *is* **cross-entropy**. That's why classifiers use it.
- Assume the target is a real number with **Gaussian noise** → negative log-likelihood *is* **mean squared error** (up to a constant). That's why regression uses MSE.
- So the two "default" losses aren't arbitrary: they are the same principle under two different assumptions about how the label was generated.

:::mint
```text
maximise  Σ log P(yᵢ | xᵢ, θ)
 categorical target  ->  minimise cross-entropy
 Gaussian target     ->  minimise MSE
```
:::
