## "Tell me about influencing a technical decision without authority."

- **What they're screening for:** leadership through evidence and persuasion — vital in AI where opinions are strong and the field is unsettled.
- **A strong answer shows:**
  - **Evidence over opinion** — you won the decision with a prototype, a benchmark on a shared eval set, or a cost analysis, not seniority or volume. In AI, "let's measure it" is the great equaliser.
  - **Understanding others' concerns** — you framed your case in terms of what each stakeholder cared about (cost, risk, timeline), not just technical elegance.
  - **A small proof** — you de-risked the disagreement with a quick spike/demo that made the better option undeniable.
  - **Bringing people along** — you built consensus rather than steamrolling, and credited others.
  - **The outcome** — the decision and its result, including graceful acceptance if you didn't fully win.
- The theme: in AI, influence comes from **running the experiment**, not from being the loudest or most senior.

:::warn
Weak: "I kept pushing until they agreed." Strong: built a quick eval/prototype that showed the better option, framed it in stakeholders' terms, and brought the team to consensus on the evidence.
:::

:::interview
What's really being tested: evidence-based influence — settling technical debates with a measurable prototype/benchmark and stakeholder-aware framing, not authority or persistence.
:::
