## "You're asked to build something technically possible but a bad idea. What do you do?"

- **What they're screening for:** judgment and communication — raising concerns constructively rather than silently building or silently refusing.
- **A strong answer shows:**
  - **Understand the real goal** — what does the requester actually want? Often there's a better way to get it.
  - **Name the concern with evidence** — cost, reliability, UX harm, maintenance burden, ethical/legal risk — specific, not vibes.
  - **Offer a better alternative** — "instead of X, Y gets you the same outcome cheaper/safer." Come with a solution, not just an objection.
  - **Disagree and commit** — if, after you've made the case, the decision stands and it's not a red line, you support it professionally (and set up monitoring to catch the risk you flagged).
  - **Escalate only for genuine red lines** (harm, legality).
- The skill: push back with data and alternatives, then either win the argument or commit gracefully.

:::warn
Weak: build it silently while resenting it, or flatly refuse. Strong: surface the concern with evidence, propose a better path, and disagree-and-commit (or escalate if it's a real red line).
:::

:::interview
What's really being tested: constructive pushback — evidence + an alternative + disagree-and-commit — distinguishing a bad idea you can voice from a red line you must escalate.
:::
