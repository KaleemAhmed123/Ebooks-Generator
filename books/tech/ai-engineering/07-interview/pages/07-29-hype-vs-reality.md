## "An executive read a headline and wants what it promised. How do you respond?"

- **What they're screening for:** managing hype without being a naysayer — translating headline capability into what's feasible for *your* context.
- **A strong answer shows:**
  - **Engage, don't dismiss** — take the underlying ambition seriously; the executive is pointing at a real opportunity even if the headline oversells.
  - **Separate the demo from your reality** — benchmark/demo results are best-case; on *our* data, latency, cost, and reliability constraints, the achievable version may differ. Say what's realistic.
  - **Offer a grounded path** — "we can get a version of this; here's a quick feasibility spike to see how close, then a staged plan." Turn hype into an experiment.
  - **Quantify tradeoffs** — "the headline version needs X budget/latency; a pragmatic version does 80% for a fraction." Give choices.
  - **Keep trust** — don't over-promise to please; being the honest broker pays off when the first over-promise would have blown up.
- The theme: redirect hype into a **feasibility experiment and a staged, honest plan**.

:::warn
Weak: "Sure, we can do exactly that" (over-promise) or "That's just hype, impossible" (dismissive). Strong: validate the ambition, state what's realistic for our constraints, and propose a feasibility spike.
:::

:::interview
What's really being tested: converting hype into a grounded, feasibility-tested plan while preserving trust — neither over-promising nor shutting the idea down.
:::
