## "Tell me about learning a new domain quickly for a project."

- **What they're screening for:** learning agility — AI engineers constantly parachute into unfamiliar domains (medical, legal, finance) and new techniques.
- **A strong answer shows:**
  - **A method, not just effort** — go top-down: the core concepts and vocabulary first, then depth where the project needs it. You don't learn the whole field, you learn the slice that matters.
  - **Leverage experts** — the fastest path is talking to domain people (and, for the tech, primary sources/docs) rather than reinventing understanding alone.
  - **Learn by building** — a quick prototype/spike surfaces what you actually need to understand far faster than passive reading.
  - **Validate your understanding** — check your mental model against an expert or a test before committing a design, so you don't build on a misconception.
  - **A concrete example** — a time you ramped on a domain, what you did, and the result.
- The theme: **targeted, source-grounded, build-to-learn**, with expert validation — not brute-force reading.

:::warn
Weak: "I read a lot and figured it out." Strong: a structured ramp — core concepts first, talk to experts/primary sources, prototype to find the real gaps, validate the mental model — with a concrete outcome.
:::
