## How does LoRA work, and why does it need so few parameters?

- **LoRA (low-rank adaptation)** freezes the pretrained weight matrix `W` and learns a small **low-rank** update beside it: `W' = W + B·A`, where `A` and `B` are skinny matrices of rank `r` (e.g. 8–64).
- A full weight matrix is `d×d` (millions of numbers). `B·A` is `d×r` + `r×d` — for small `r`, a **tiny fraction** of the parameters. You train only `A` and `B`.
- Why it works: fine-tuning moves the weights along a **low-dimensional** subspace — the needed update has low "intrinsic rank," so a low-rank matrix captures it well.
- Wins: ~0.1–1% of parameters trained, so small memory and fast training; adapters are **tiny files** you can swap or stack per task; the base model is shared across many adapters (one GPU serves many fine-tunes).
