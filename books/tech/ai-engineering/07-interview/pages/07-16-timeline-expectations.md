## "How do you manage timeline expectations on an AI project?"

- **What they're screening for:** realism about AI's inherent uncertainty — you can't always know up front whether the model can hit the bar.
- **A strong answer shows:**
  - **Separate the knowable from the research-y** — plumbing/integration is estimable; "can the model reach 95% on this task" is a research question with uncertain timing. Call that out.
  - **De-risk early** — do a quick **feasibility spike** against an eval set *before* committing a timeline, so you're estimating on evidence, not hope.
  - **Stage the plan** — feasibility → thin slice → iterate to the quality bar → harden. Communicate milestones with confidence levels, not a single false-precise date.
  - **Communicate continuously** — update stakeholders as evidence comes in; renegotiate scope if the quality bar proves harder than expected (ship the achievable slice).
- The theme: estimate after a feasibility check, stage the work, and be honest that quality is uncertain until measured.

:::warn
Weak: "I'll have the AI feature done in two weeks" (false precision on an unknown). Strong: "Give me a few days to spike feasibility against an eval set, then I'll give a staged estimate with confidence levels."
:::
