## How does DPO align a model without a reward model?

- **DPO (direct preference optimization)** skips the separate reward model and RL loop. It uses the same preference pairs (chosen vs rejected) but optimises the LLM **directly** with a simple classification-style loss.
- The insight: the optimal RLHF policy has a closed-form relationship to the reward, so you can rewrite the objective to depend only on the policy and a frozen reference model. The loss pushes up the probability of the chosen response and down the rejected one, **scaled by how much they've moved from the reference**.
- Benefits: **two models not four** (policy + frozen reference), stable supervised-style training, no reward model to build or game, no on-policy sampling.
- Cost: it's **off-policy** (learns from a fixed dataset, not fresh samples), can over-optimise on the preference data, and its ceiling depends entirely on that dataset's quality.

:::mint
```text
DPO loss ∝ −log σ( β·[ log π(chosen)/π_ref(chosen) − log π(rejected)/π_ref(rejected) ] )
```
:::

What's really being tested:

that DPO folds the reward into a direct supervised loss on preference pairs (2 models, stable), and the tradeoff that it's off-policy and dataset-bound.
