## What is Reciprocal Rank Fusion, and why is it the default way to merge result lists?

- When you have two ranked lists (e.g. dense and BM25), you can't just add their scores — they're on **incompatible scales** (cosine similarity vs BM25 term weights). Normalising scores is fiddly and fragile.
- **RRF (reciprocal rank fusion)** sidesteps scores entirely and fuses by **rank**: each document gets `Σ 1/(k + rank)` summed across the lists it appears in (k ≈ 60). A document ranked high in either list rises; appearing in both boosts it.
- Why it's popular: **no score calibration**, robust across retrievers, trivially extends to 3+ lists, and empirically strong. It just needs each system's ordering.
- The `k` constant dampens the influence of top ranks so lower ranks still contribute; 60 is the common default.

:::mint
```text
RRF(d) = Σ  1 / (k + rankᵢ(d))      k ≈ 60, over each list i containing d
```
:::

:::interview
What's really being tested: that RRF fuses by rank (not score) so you avoid calibrating incompatible scales — the practical reason it's the default fusion in hybrid search.
:::
