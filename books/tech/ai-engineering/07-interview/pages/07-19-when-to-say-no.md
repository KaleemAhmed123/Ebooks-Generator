## "Tell me about a time you'd say no to an AI use case."

- **What they're screening for:** ethical judgment and the backbone to push back — increasingly important as AI touches high-stakes decisions.
- **A strong answer shows:**
  - **Recognising red lines** — use cases where AI shouldn't make the call: high-stakes decisions without human oversight (medical, legal, hiring, lending) made *autonomously*, deceptive uses, surveillance, or anything where a wrong answer causes serious harm and can't be caught.
  - **A constructive reframe** — rather than a flat refusal, propose a safer version: AI as decision *support* with a human in the loop, narrower scope, stronger guardrails, or clear disclosure.
  - **Grounding in principles and risk** — tie it to responsible-AI practice and (where relevant) regulation, not just personal discomfort.
  - **Escalation** — raise concerns to the right people rather than quietly complying or quietly sabotaging.
- Pick a concrete example and show you balanced business pressure against responsibility.

:::warn
Weak: "I'd do whatever the business wants" or "I'd just refuse." Strong: identify the harm/red line, propose a safer scoped alternative (human-in-the-loop, disclosure), and escalate properly.
:::

:::interview
What's really being tested: ethical backbone with pragmatism — spotting harmful/high-stakes uses and redirecting to a safer design, not blind compliance or unhelpful refusal.
:::
