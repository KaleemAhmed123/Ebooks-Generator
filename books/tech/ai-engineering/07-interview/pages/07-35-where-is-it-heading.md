## "Where do you think AI engineering is heading — or a strong opinion you hold?"

- **What they're screening for:** that you think critically about the field, hold **reasoned** opinions, and can defend them — not that you predict the future correctly.
- **A strong answer shows:**
  - **A specific, defensible take** rather than vague optimism — e.g. "evaluation is becoming the core skill, not prompting," "agents will win where tasks are verifiable and stall where they aren't," "most value is in the application layer and data, not training foundation models," or "context/retrieval engineering matters more than model choice for most products."
  - **Reasoning from evidence** — ground it in what you've seen work and fail, and acknowledge uncertainty and the counter-argument. Strong opinions, loosely held.
  - **Practical implications** — what it means for how you'd build, which shows you think like a practitioner, not a pundit.
  - **Intellectual honesty** — you distinguish what you believe from what's proven, and you'd update on evidence.
- The point is the *quality of reasoning*, not the specific prediction.

:::warn
Weak: "AI will keep getting better and change everything." Strong: a specific thesis (e.g. "evals, not prompting, are the durable skill"), the evidence for it, the counter-case, and what it changes about how you build.
:::

:::interview
What's really being tested: critical thinking and a defensible, evidence-grounded point of view held with intellectual honesty — the ability to reason about the field, not to be a correct oracle.
:::
