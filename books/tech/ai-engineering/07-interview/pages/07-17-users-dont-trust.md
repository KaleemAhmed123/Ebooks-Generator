## "Users don't trust your AI feature. How do you fix that?"

- **What they're screening for:** UX and product thinking about trust — adoption, not just accuracy, decides whether an AI feature succeeds.
- **A strong answer shows:**
  - **Diagnose the distrust** — is it actually wrong (quality), or right-but-unexplained (transparency), or inconsistent (reliability)? Fix the real cause.
  - **Build trust through design:**
    - **Show your work** — citations/sources, so users can verify (huge for RAG).
    - **Express uncertainty** — hedge or defer when unsure rather than confidently wrong; "I don't know" builds trust.
    - **Keep the user in control** — let them edit/override/undo; AI as assist, not autopilot, where stakes are real.
    - **Be consistent and fast** — erratic or slow responses read as untrustworthy.
  - **Earn trust incrementally** — start with low-stakes assist, prove reliability with data, expand scope.
- The insight: **trust is a product feature**, driven by transparency, control, and consistency — not just a higher accuracy number.

:::warn
Weak: "Make the model more accurate." Strong: "Accuracy helps, but citations, honest uncertainty, user control, and consistency are what actually build trust — and I'd start low-stakes and earn it."
:::

:::interview
What's really being tested: that you treat trust as a UX/product problem (transparency, uncertainty, control, consistency), not solely a model-accuracy problem.
:::
