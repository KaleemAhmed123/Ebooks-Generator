## Recognition drills after Chapter 16 <span class="lv lv2"></span>

Name the node, the edge and the move before the algorithm. Answers overleaf.

1. Stones sit on grid points, one per point. A stone may be removed while another remaining stone shares its row or column. Remove as many as possible.
2. A permutation of 0 to n − 1: follow `i → a[i] → a[a[i]] …` until a value repeats. Return the longest such walk.
3. Courses have durations and prerequisite pairs; any number may run at once. Return the minimum months to finish them all.
4. A grid of land and water: count the land regions that touch no edge of the grid.
5. Words are all anagrams of one another. Two are linked if swapping two letters turns one into the other. Count the linked groups.
6. A directed graph: list the nodes from which every walk ends at a node with no outgoing edges.
7. Each entry is the longest jump allowed from that index. Can you reach the last index?
8. A tree on n nodes gained one extra edge. Return an edge whose removal leaves a tree.
9. A height map touches one ocean on its top and left edges and another on its bottom and right. Water flows to any neighbour no higher. List the cells that drain to both oceans.
10. Words are sorted in an unknown alphabet. Recover an order of its letters.
11. A string and pairs of positions whose characters may be swapped any number of times. Return the smallest string reachable.
12. Up to 100 courses with prerequisite pairs, where prerequisites are transitive, and 10⁴ queries "must u come before v?".
