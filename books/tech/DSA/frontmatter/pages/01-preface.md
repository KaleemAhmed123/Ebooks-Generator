## Preface

When I first started preparing for coding interviews, I fell into the same trap as everyone else. I memorized the "Sliding Window" template. I memorized the "Two Pointers" template. I memorized how to write Dijkstra's algorithm.

And then I went into the interview, saw a problem that didn't perfectly match any of my templates, and failed.

The industry has treated Data Structures and Algorithms as a trivia contest. We are told to grind hundreds of problems until we can recognize them on sight. But this approach is brittle. When the constraints change, or the problem is slightly shifted, memorization shatters.

This book exists because I wanted to teach the *derivation* of algorithms, not just their final form. 

### Derive, Don't Memorize

Every algorithm in this book is presented not as a magical block of code, but as a logical conclusion. We start with the brute force. We ask why it is slow. We identify what work is being repeated. We find a data structure to remember that work. And suddenly, the optimal algorithm writes itself.

When you learn to derive algorithms, you don't need to memorize 500 LeetCode problems. You just need to understand 15 core patterns and the mathematical realities of time complexity.

### Who This Book Is For

This book is for the engineer who is tired of feeling like they are "guessing" the right approach. It is for the candidate aiming for Google, Amazon, or high-growth startups who wants to walk into an interview and build a solution from first principles.

The final module (Module 08) is for those who want to push further, into the realm of Competitive Programming, where the constraints are brutal and the math is unforgiving.

### How to Read It

You can read this cover-to-cover, but I recommend treating it as a reference manual.
- **Green markers (🟢)** indicate foundational concepts required for every interview.
- **Yellow markers (🟡)** indicate advanced patterns expected at top-tier companies.
- **Red markers (🔴)** indicate competitive programming techniques that are rarely asked in standard interviews.

Some of the code in here will inevitably have edge-case bugs. Algorithms don't change, but language updates and edge cases do. If you find a flaw, I invite you to correct it. Reach out to me at **kaleemahmed.in**.

Let's start deriving.
