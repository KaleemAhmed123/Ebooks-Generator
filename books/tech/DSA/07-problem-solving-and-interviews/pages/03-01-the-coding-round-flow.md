## The Coding Round Flow

A 45-minute technical interview is not a test of your ability to write code. It is a simulation of how you work with a colleague on a difficult problem.

If you sit in silence for 20 minutes, write a flawless O(N) solution, and say "Done," you will likely fail the interview. The interviewer learned nothing about your communication skills, your ability to handle ambiguity, or your thought process.

### The 45-Minute Breakdown

You must explicitly manage the time and guide the interviewer through this flow:

1. **Understand and Clarify (3-5 minutes):** Listen to the prompt. Repeat it back. Ask about edge cases. Ask for the constraints.
2. **Brute Force (2-3 minutes):** Propose the simplest, dumbest solution out loud. Prove you can at least solve it.
3. **Optimise and Prove (10-15 minutes):** Brainstorm algorithms. Map constraints to structures. Propose the optimal approach and state its time/space complexity. **Get explicit agreement to start coding.**
4. **Implement Cleanly (15-20 minutes):** Write the code. Talk while you type. Keep functions modular.
5. **Dry Run (5 minutes):** Do not click "Run" (if in an IDE) or wait for the interviewer to find bugs. Manually trace the code with a small example.
6. **Follow-ups (Remaining time):** Discuss how the system would scale, or how to handle larger constraints.

### The Signal

The interviewer is checking a rubric:
- **Problem Solving:** Can they translate a vague requirement into a rigorous algorithm?
- **Coding:** Are they fluent in their language? Do they use standard libraries correctly?
- **Verification:** Can they find their own bugs?
- **Communication:** Would I want to pair-program with this person?
