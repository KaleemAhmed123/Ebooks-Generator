## The Google Style

Google interviews are famous for being highly theoretical and heavily focused on algorithms rather than frameworks. You will rarely be asked to parse a JSON file or build a React component in a Google algorithmic round.

### The Reasoning Type

Google tests **Derivation**. They actively try to invent questions that cannot be found on LeetCode. They want to see what happens when you encounter a problem you have never seen before.

- **Heavy on Math and DP:** You are highly likely to encounter problems requiring mathematical reasoning, probability, or inventing a complex Dynamic Programming state.
- **Heavy on Graph Modeling:** They love problems that don't look like graphs but must be modeled as graphs (e.g., state-space searches, word transformations).
- **The Follow-up:** "Can you do it in O(N)?" Google interviewers will almost always push you for the absolute theoretical minimum time and space complexity. 

### How to Succeed

- **Do not jump to coding.** If you start coding a sub-optimal solution immediately, you will fail. Spend the first 15 minutes at the whiteboard (or virtual doc) proving the optimal algorithm.
- **Master the Unnamed Patterns:** Google relies heavily on patterns like *Dominated Candidate Elimination* (Monotonic Stacks) and *Boundary Finding* (Binary Search on Answer). 
- **Explain your failures:** If you try an approach and realize it's wrong, explicitly state *why* it's mathematically flawed before pivoting. They grade your analytical pivot.
