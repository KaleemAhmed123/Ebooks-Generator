## PodDisruptionBudgets

- Pods die two ways, and you control only one. **Involuntary disruptions** — a node crashes, a kernel panics, the hardware fails — are unpredictable; you survive them with replicas and anti-affinity (Module 3.4). **Voluntary disruptions** — a `kubectl drain` for a node upgrade, a cluster autoscaler removing a node, a rolling node replacement — are initiated *by you or your tooling*, and that's where a **PodDisruptionBudget (PDB)** gives you a say.
- A PDB sets a floor on availability during voluntary disruptions: `minAvailable: 2` (or `maxUnavailable: 1`) tells the system **"never let a voluntary action take me below this."** When something tries to evict a pod — a drain — it must respect the budget: it evicts only if enough pods stay up, otherwise it **waits**.

- Without a PDB, draining the node that happens to hold all 3 replicas of a service evicts them **simultaneously** — a self-inflicted outage during a routine upgrade. With `minAvailable: 2`, the drain evicts one, waits for its replacement to come up elsewhere and pass readiness, then takes the next. The maintenance still completes; it just can't breach your availability floor.
