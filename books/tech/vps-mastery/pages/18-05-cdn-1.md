## A CDN in front

- A **CDN** caches responses at locations near users. For a single-box deployment it changes three things

| Gain | Effect |
|---|---|
| Static assets served from the edge | Bandwidth and CPU leave the box |
| The origin address is hidden | Direct attacks on the IP become harder |
| TLS terminated at the edge | Faster handshake for distant users |

### The two settings that matter on day one

- **Cache everything under the hashed asset path.** `/_next/static/` and equivalents are immutable and safe to cache for a year
- **Cache nothing under `/api/`.** A cached authenticated response served to the wrong user is the worst possible outcome

```text
/_next/static/*   cache 1 year
/images/*         cache 30 days
/api/*            bypass
/                 bypass, or cache by cookie presence
```

### The header trap

- Behind a CDN, every request arrives from the CDN's address. Rate limiting and logging both break
- Restore the real address, page 07-15:

```nginx
set_real_ip_from 173.245.48.0/20;
real_ip_header CF-Connecting-IP;
```

- **Only trust the header from the CDN's own ranges.** Trusting it from anywhere lets a client claim any address
