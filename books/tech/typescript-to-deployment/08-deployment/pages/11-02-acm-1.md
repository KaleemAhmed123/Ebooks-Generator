## Certificates

- **ACM issues and renews TLS certificates at no cost**, and renewal is automatic as long as the validation record stays in place
- The certificate is attached to a load balancer or a CloudFront distribution. **The private key is never downloadable**, which is a feature

```bash
aws acm request-certificate \
  --domain-name api.example.com \
  --subject-alternative-names '*.example.com' \
  --validation-method DNS \
  --key-algorithm EC_prime256v1

aws acm describe-certificate --certificate-arn <arn> \
  --query 'Certificate.DomainValidationOptions[].ResourceRecord'

aws acm list-certificates --certificate-statuses ISSUED PENDING_VALIDATION
```

### DNS validation, and why it is the right choice

- ACM gives a `CNAME` record to add. Once it resolves, the certificate is issued, usually within minutes
- **Leave that record in place forever.** ACM renews automatically only while it can still validate, and removing it means a certificate expires with no warning
- Email validation exists and requires a human to click a link every renewal. Do not use it

### The two rules that cost people an afternoon

- **A certificate for CloudFront must be issued in `us-east-1`**, whatever region everything else is in. There is no exception
- **A certificate for an ALB must be in the ALB's region**
- A distribution serving `example.com` and `api.example.com` therefore often needs two certificates in two regions
