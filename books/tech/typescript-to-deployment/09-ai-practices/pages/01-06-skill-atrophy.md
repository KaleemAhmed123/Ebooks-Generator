## Keeping the skill

- The concern is real and it is worth stating plainly: **a skill you stop exercising degrades**, and delegating every implementation degrades implementation skill
- The research on this describes it as a hidden cost of adoption, alongside the verification tax. It is not a reason to avoid the tools; it is a reason to be deliberate

### Where it actually bites

- **Debugging.** Finding a bug requires holding a mental model of the system. Generated code you skim leaves no model behind
- **Judgement.** Knowing that an approach will hurt in six months comes from having been hurt by it
- **Depth in a new area.** Delegating your way through unfamiliar territory means you finish without having learned it

### What to keep doing by hand

| Keep | Because |
|---|---|
| **the core domain logic** | it is where the value and the subtlety live |
| **anything you are learning** | delegating the learning skips the learning |
| **the debugging** | ask it to explain, then find the bug yourself |
| **the design** | the decision, always. It can draft, you decide |

### The habit that preserves it

- **Read the diff properly, every time.** Reviewing carefully is itself practice, and it is the practice that matters most now
- **Ask why, not just what.** "Why this approach over the other" turns a delegation into a lesson
- **Occasionally write it first, then compare.** The comparison teaches more than either alone
