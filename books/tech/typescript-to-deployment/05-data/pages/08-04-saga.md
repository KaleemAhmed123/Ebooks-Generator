## Sagas

- A transaction guarantees all-or-nothing inside one database. Across three services there is no such guarantee
- Charging a card, reserving stock and creating a shipment live in three places, and a failure at step three cannot roll back step one
- A **saga** replaces rollback with **compensation**. Each step has an action and an undo, and failure runs the undos backwards

| Step | Action | Compensation |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | charge the card | refund the charge |
| 2 | reserve stock | release the reservation |
| 3 | create the shipment | cancel the shipment |

### Two ways to run one

- **Choreography.** Each service listens for the previous event and emits the next. No coordinator, and no single place shows the whole flow
- **Orchestration.** One service owns the sequence and calls each step. Easier to see and to debug, and it is a component that can itself fail
- Choreography suits three steps. Orchestration suits seven

### What makes them hard

- **A compensation is not a rollback.** A refund is a new transaction, visible on a statement, and the money was really gone for a while
- **Some steps cannot be undone.** An email is sent. A physical parcel has left. Put those last, after everything reversible has succeeded
- **Compensations fail too**, and a failed compensation needs a human, so it must raise an alert rather than a log line
- **Intermediate states are visible.** Between steps two and three the order is genuinely half-created, and the interface has to say something honest about that
