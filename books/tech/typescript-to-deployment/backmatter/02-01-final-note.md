# A final note

## You got to the end

- That is 1,200 pages and change. Whether you read it front to back or opened one booklet the night before a project, thank you for the time

### What to do next

- **Pick the booklet closest to what you are building this month and re-read it properly.** A skim tells you what exists; a second pass is where it sticks
- **Take one thing from Booklet 8 and do it today.** Set a log retention policy, or a budget alarm. Both default to unbounded and both are only noticed on an invoice
- **Write the twenty evaluation cases** from Booklet 7 or 9 for whatever you are shipping. Twenty run on every change beats two hundred written once and never run

### Where this will be wrong

- **Versions move.** Every command here was checked against a real installation on the date stamped at the end of each booklet. Some of it is already out of date, and the pages tell you to verify rather than trust
- **The AI booklets age fastest.** The tool names in Booklet 9 will change within a year. The loop underneath them will not
- **Some of it is opinion.** ECS over Kubernetes, Caddy before Nginx, pino over Winston. The reasoning is written down so you can disagree with the reasoning, not just the conclusion
