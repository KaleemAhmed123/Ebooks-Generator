# About the author

**Kaleem Ahmed** is a backend engineer. He works mostly in Node.js, TypeScript
and Python, on the kind of systems that have to keep running long after the
demo: integrations, data synchronisation, document automation, real-time
features, AI-backed products, and the infrastructure underneath all of it.

Most of what is in these pages was learned the ordinary way. Something broke,
or came close to breaking, and the fix turned out to need a layer he had been
content to ignore until then. Queues taught him about duplicate messages.
Databases taught him about isolation levels. Deployments taught him that the
interesting failures happen on a Sunday.

He writes things down because writing is where he finds out whether he actually
understood something. An explanation that reads cleanly usually means the idea
is settled; one that keeps needing another clause usually means it is not. Much
of this book is the second kind, rewritten until it became the first.

His aim when explaining is narrow: remove the unnecessary complexity without
removing the necessary complexity. A good explanation should make a hard thing
approachable while being honest that the hard part is still there.

He is not certain about all of it. Some pages describe decisions he would make
differently today, and the reasoning is written down precisely so it can be
argued with rather than copied. If something here is wrong, out of date, or
badly explained, he would rather hear about it than not.

Away from a keyboard he travels, plays snooker, and works through puzzles for
no better reason than that they are interesting. Puzzles, unfamiliar cities and
complicated systems reward the same three things: curiosity, patience, and
being willing to say that you do not understand something yet.
