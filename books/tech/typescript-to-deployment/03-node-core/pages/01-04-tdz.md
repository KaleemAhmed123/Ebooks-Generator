## The temporal dead zone

- `let` and `const` are hoisted too, but you cannot touch them before the declaration line
- That gap is called the **temporal dead zone**

```js
console.log(a)   // undefined
var a = 1

console.log(b)   // ReferenceError: Cannot access 'b' before initialization
let b = 2
```

- The error text is the giveaway. It says *cannot access*, not *is not defined*
- *Is not defined* means the name does not exist at all
- *Cannot access before initialization* means it exists and you are early

### Why this is better

- `var` gave you `undefined` and let the program keep going with a wrong value
- `let` stops immediately at the line that is actually wrong

:::note
`typeof` normally never throws. On a variable in the temporal dead zone it does. That is the one exception.
:::
