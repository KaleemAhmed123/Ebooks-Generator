## NestJS

- A small Express service is a folder of routes. A large one is forty files with no agreed shape
- Every team invents its own structure, and a new joiner spends their first week learning that structure rather than the domain
- NestJS supplies the structure instead of leaving it to you, borrowing its model from Angular and from Java's Spring
- The core mechanism is **dependency injection**: a class lists what it needs in its constructor, and a container supplies it
- That inversion is what makes a service testable, because a test can hand it a fake repository without the class knowing
- Modules, guards, interceptors, pipes and filters each give one cross-cutting concern a named place to live
- Auth stops being a middleware someone forgot to add, and becomes a guard the framework applies
- The cost is real. Decorators and DI take weeks to sit comfortably, and every feature is more files
- On a service with six endpoints, the structure costs more than it returns
