## What the 2026 Interview Actually Looks Like

The two pages before this cover system design and behavioral answers. This one
is about the shape of the process itself, because it changed, and the change is
not evenly reported.

### Five stages

The common structure at companies large enough to have one:

1. **Recruiter screen.** Salary band, notice period, whether the role is what
   the advert said.
2. **Technical screen.** 45 to 60 minutes. A small coding problem or a
   conversation about your last project.
3. **Coding round.** Take-home or live. Usually building or debugging a
   component.
4. **Frontend system design.** The round that separates levels.
5. **Behavioral.** With the hiring manager, and it is not a formality.

The weight moved to stage 4. With implementation getting cheaper, the questions
that decide the outcome are architectural: what you cache, what you stream, what
you would give up first.

### The AI question, which you must ask

This is the biggest single change and the one candidates get wrong.

**Whether you may use an AI assistant is now a per-company policy, not an
industry rule.** A Karat survey in January 2026 found **62% of organizations
still prohibit AI in technical interviews**. Meanwhile **Meta and Google are
piloting rounds where an assistant is provided and your use of it is graded.**

Both extremes exist at once. Guessing wrong costs you the round in either
direction: use it where it is banned and you are done, refuse to use it where it
is expected and you look like you cannot work the way the team works.

**Ask the recruiter, in writing, before the coding round.** Nobody has ever
thought worse of a candidate for asking.
