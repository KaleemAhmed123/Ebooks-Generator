# A final note

## You got to the end

- That is 532 pages across six booklets. Whether you read it in order or opened one the week you had to build the thing, thank you for the time

### What to do next

- **Take the design you are actually working on and name its promises.** Every box you have drawn: what does it guarantee, what does it refuse, and which number did you pick without checking
- **Find one default you have inherited and never read.** A timeout, a retry count, a replication mode, a TTL. Most outages in these pages started as a default that nobody had a reason for
- **Work one of Booklet 6's eighteen designs on paper, out loud, against a clock.** Knowing the mechanism and being able to assemble it under pressure are different skills, and only the second one gets tested

### Where this will be wrong

- **Numbers move.** Every default, limit and latency here was checked against a primary source as this edition was built. Some of it is already stale, and the pages tell you which claims are load-bearing enough to re-check
- **Some of it is a judgement call.** When to accept eventual consistency, when a saga beats a distributed transaction, when a monolith is the right answer. The reasoning is written down so you can disagree with the reasoning rather than the conclusion
- **Some subjects are missing on purpose, and one is missing with regret.** Geospatial indexing — geohash, quadtrees, H3 — is commonly asked and is not here. It needs a module of its own rather than a few pages appended to something else
