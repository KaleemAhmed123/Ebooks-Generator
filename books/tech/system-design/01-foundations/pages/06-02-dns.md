## DNS — a record with a TTL

- The browser (or OS resolver) asks a DNS server for the IP address behind a domain name. The answer is cached for the **TTL** (time to live) — typically 60–300 seconds
- Failover by changing a DNS record takes at least one TTL for every resolver to pick up the new address. Some resolvers ignore TTL and cache longer

### What this means for failover

- A DNS-based failover with a 300 s TTL means up to 5 minutes before all clients see the new IP. During those 5 minutes, some clients hit the old (possibly dead) address
- AWS's 2025 us-east-1 incident (Module 2, page 6) was an empty DNS record. The service behind it was healthy; the name that resolved to it was not

### The failure

- A 300 s TTL on the record you plan to flip in an emergency. The flip takes 30 seconds. The propagation takes 5 minutes. The SLO allows 4 minutes. The DNS cache is the blocker
